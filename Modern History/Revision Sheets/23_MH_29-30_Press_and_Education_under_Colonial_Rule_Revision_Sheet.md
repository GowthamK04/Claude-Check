## 1. The spine
Both were **built for rule**: press tolerated for commerce and watched for sedition; education funded to make clerks. Press control **tightened after every crisis, relaxed in calm**. Education moved from **filtration (1835)** to **mass schooling on paper (1854)**, never in money — **~18% literate at the 1951 census**. Indians **appropriated both** → nationalism, led by a **narrow** English-educated class.

## 2. Press laws — tighten and relax

| Year | Ruler | Measure | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1780** | — | **Hicky's Bengal Gazette** — first paper; seized 1782 | start |
| **1799** | Wellesley | Censorship of Press Act (fear of the French) | tighten |
| **1823** | John Adam | Licensing Regulations; Rammohun closes *Mirat-ul-Akbar* | tighten |
| **1835** | **Metcalfe** | Press Act — only a declaration; **"liberator of the Indian press"** | relax |
| **1857** | Canning | Licensing Act (one year) | tighten |
| **1867** | — | Press and Registration of Books Act — regulate, not restrict (replaced by PRP Act **2023**) | neutral |
| **1878** | **Lytton** | **Vernacular Press Act** — vernacular papers only; bonds; confiscation; **no appeal**; "gagging Act" | tighten |
| **1882** | **Ripon** | VPA **repealed** | relax |
| **1908** | Minto | Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act — presses confiscated | tighten |
| **1910** | — | Indian Press Act — security deposits revived | tighten |
| **1921** | — | **Sapru Press Committee** → 1908 and 1910 Acts repealed | relax |
| **1931** | — | Press (Emergency Powers) Act — Civil Disobedience; widened 1932 | tighten |
| **1939–45** | — | Defence of India Rules; pre-censorship; closures in 1942 | tighten |

- **VPA:** *Amrita Bazar Patrika* became an English paper overnight to escape it.
- **Tilak:** tried 1897 (18 months) and **1908 (six years, Mandalay)** for *Kesari* articles → Bombay mill workers' strike.

> **Line:** "Every major press law followed a nationalist advance."

## 5. Orientalists vs Anglicists; Macaulay (2026)
- **Orientalists:** H. T. Prinsep, H. H. Wilson. **Anglicists:** Macaulay, Charles Trevelyan, missionaries; **Rammohun's 1823 letter** for Western science.
- **Minute, 2 February 1835** — quote exactly:
  - "a class of persons, Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect" — interpreters "between us and the millions whom we govern".
  - "a single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia".
- **Bentinck's Resolution, 7 March 1835** · **downward filtration theory**.
- **Administration:** English the language of rule · **Persian replaced in courts 1837** · **Hardinge 1844** prefers English-educated for posts → *babus*.
- **Education:** English colleges in presidency towns; oriental and vernacular schooling starved.
- **Unintended:** the interpreter class became the nationalist class.
- **Gandhi, *Hind Swaraj* (1909):** to give millions a knowledge of English is to enslave them.
- Vernacular alternative: **Adam's Reports 1835–38**; **Thomason** in NWP 1843–53.

> **Line:** "Macaulay set out to create loyal interpreters and created the leaders of the national movement."

## 6. The policy ladder
| Step | Key content |
|---|---|
| **Wood's Despatch 1854** — "Magna Carta of English education" | State responsibility for mass education · **vernacular primary, English higher** · village schools → high schools → colleges · **universities 1857** (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras; London model) · **grants-in-aid** · departments of public instruction · teacher training · women's education · secular |
| **Hunter Commission 1882–83** | Primary to district and municipal boards · secondary literary + vocational · private enterprise, women |
| **Raleigh 1902 → Indian Universities Act 1904** (Curzon) | Official control of senates, affiliation; government veto |
| **Gokhale's Bill 1911** | Compulsory primary education — defeated (1912) |
| **1913 Resolution** | Illiteracy removal accepted; no compulsion |
| **Sadler 1917–19** | 12-year school · intermediate colleges · 3-year degree · unitary residential universities |
| **Montford 1919** | Education transferred to provincial Indian ministers |
| **Hartog 1929** | "Wastage and stagnation" · consolidate primary · vocational after middle school |
| **Wardha 1937** | **Nai Talim** · Zakir Hussain Committee · 7 years free and compulsory · mother tongue · craft-centred, self-supporting · stalled 1939 |
| **Sargent 1944** | Pre-primary 3–6 · free compulsory **6–14** · academic and technical high schools · 3-year degree · **40-year plan** |

## 7. National education
- **Reformers:** MAO College Aligarh **1875** · Deccan Education Society **1884** (Fergusson 1885) · DAV Lahore **1886** · BHU **1916** · Karve's women's university **1916**.
- **Swadeshi:** **National Council of Education 1906** · Bengal National College (Aurobindo principal) · Bengal Technical Institute.
- **Non-Cooperation:** Gujarat Vidyapith **1920** · Jamia Millia Islamia **1920** (Delhi 1925) · Kashi Vidyapith **1921** · Bihar Vidyapith · Lajpat Rai's National School, Lahore.
- **Tagore:** Santiniketan **1901** · Visva-Bharati **1921** · Sriniketan **1922**.

## 8. Gandhi vs Tagore (2023)
| Axis | Gandhi | Tagore |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Character, self-reliance — "head, heart and hand" | Freedom of mind, creativity, nature |
| Method | Productive craft; self-supporting | Arts, open air; no rote |
| Medium | Mother tongue | Mother tongue; open to world cultures |
| Institutions | Gujarat Vidyapith; Wardha 1937 | Santiniketan; Visva-Bharati |
| Nationalism | Mass, moral, anti-colonial; school boycott 1920–21 | Critic of nationalism (*Nationalism*, 1917); internationalist |
| Clash | "The Great Sentinel" (1921) | "The Call of Truth" (1921) |
| Common | Anti-rote, mother tongue, village reconstruction | same |

> **Line:** "Gandhi sought a free nation, Tagore a free mind."

## 9. Assessment
- **Achieved:** liberal ideas; a common English-language elite across regions; leaders of Congress and reform.
- **Failed:** mass literacy (**~18% at 1951 census**); women's, technical, scientific education; urban, upper-caste, examination-driven.
- **Historiography:** **Gauri Viswanathan, *Masks of Conquest* (1989)** — English literature as control · **Bernard Cohn (1996)** — knowledge as a tool of rule · **Krishna Kumar (1991)** — political agenda of education.

## The traps
1. VPA = **Lytton**, not Curzon; **Ripon** repealed.
2. Fort William = **Richard** Wellesley, for **British** civilians.
3. Benaras Sanskrit College = **Jonathan Duncan**; William Jones = Asiatic Society.
4. Wood = English **only at higher levels**.
5. Hunter **Commission** 1882 (education) ≠ Hunter **Committee** 1919 (Jallianwala).
6. 1904 Act = **universities**.
7. Bethune School founded by **J. E. D. Bethune** (1849); Vidyasagar was secretary — but the 2005 key credits Vidyasagar.
