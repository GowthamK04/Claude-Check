## 1. The spine
**Local enemy, legal method** (to 1917) → **Gandhi links grievance to nation** (1917–22) → **unions and the left** (1920s–30s) → **class adjustment** by Congress (Karachi 1931, Faizpur 1936, 1937 ministries) → **1940s revolt** (Quit India villages, Tebhaga, Telangana, 1946 strikes).

> **Line:** "Before 1917 the peasant's enemy was the planter, zamindar or moneylender; after Gandhi it was also the Raj."

---

## 2. Peasants — early phase, 1857–1917
| Movement | Where · when | Against · leaders | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indigo Revolt** | Bengal · **1859–60** | European planters · Digambar and Bishnu Biswas (Nadia) · *Nil Darpan* (**Dinabandhu Mitra**, 1860) · *Hindoo Patriot* | **Indigo Commission 1860**; planters shift to Bihar |
| **Pabna leagues** | East Bengal · **1873–76** | Zamindars' illegal rent enhancement · Ishan Chandra Roy, Shambhu Pal · legal rent strikes · backed by Bankim, R. C. Dutt, Indian Association | **Bengal Tenancy Act 1885** |
| **Deccan Riots** | Poona, Ahmednagar · **1875** | Marwari/Gujarati moneylenders after cotton slump · social boycott, debt bonds burnt | **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act 1879** |
| **Punjab agitation** | **1907** | Colonisation Bill, water rates · **Ajit Singh, Lajpat Rai** · "Pagri Sambhal Jatta" | Bill vetoed; leaders deported |

**Features:** enemy local, not the Raj · legal methods, faith in British courts · no all-India body · intellectuals sympathised, did not lead.

## 3. Peasants — Gandhian phase and Kisan Sabhas, 1917–1939
- **Champaran 1917** — *tinkathia* (indigo on **3/20**) · Rajkumar Shukla · Rajendra Prasad, J. B. Kripalani, Mazharul Haq · Champaran Agrarian Act 1918.
- **Kheda 1918** — revenue not suspended despite crop failure · **Patel**.
- **UP Kisan Sabha, Feb 1918** — **Gauri Shankar Mishra, Indra Narain Dwivedi**, Malaviya's support · **not Nehru**.
- **Awadh Kisan Sabha, Oct 1920** — **Baba Ramchandra**, Nehru, Gauri Shankar Mishra · against *nazrana*, *begar*, eviction.
- **Eka 1921–22** — **Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur** · **Madari Pasi** · rents >50% above recorded · began with Congress/Khilafat, then autonomous; suppressed.
- **Mappila 1921** — Malabar · Muslim tenants vs Hindu *jenmis* · Khilafat · communal turn · wagon tragedy.
- **Bardoli 1928** — revenue hike **~22%** · **Patel** ("Sardar") · **Maxwell–Broomfield** inquiry → **~6%**.
- **1930–32** — no-revenue (Gujarat, Andhra), no-rent (UP), forest satyagraha.
- **All-India Kisan Sabha — Lucknow, April 1936** — president **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati**, general secretary **N. G. Ranga**; Indulal Yagnik · **Kisan Manifesto (Aug 1936):** zamindari abolition, rent/revenue cuts, debt cancellation, minimum wages.
- **Faizpur 1936** agrarian programme · **ministries 1937–39** tenancy and debt laws · **Bakasht** struggle, Bihar (Sahajanand, Karyanand Sharma) · Ranga's peasant institute, Nidubrolu (1933).

## 4. Peasants — the 1940s
| Movement | Where · when | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Quit India** | Bihar, E. UP, Midnapore, Satara · 1942 | Parallel governments: **Ballia, Tamluk, Satara** · Linlithgow: most serious since 1857 |
| **Warli** | Thane · 1945 | **Godavari Parulekar** |
| **Punnapra-Vayalar** | Travancore · 1946 | Coir workers, communists vs Dewan **C. P. Ramaswamy Iyer** |
| **Tebhaga** | Bengal · **1946–47** | *Bargadars* claim two-thirds → landlord's share **½ → ⅓** · Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha · **Floud Commission 1940** · Bargadars Bill 1947 |
| **Telangana** | Hyderabad · **1946–51** | Vs Nizam's *deshmukhs*, *vetti* · communist village soviets · ended 1951 after Police Action 1948 |

## 5. Peasants — assessment
- **Contribution:** mass base; land reform and zamindari abolition on the agenda.
- **Limits:** rich/middle-peasant leadership; sharecroppers and labourers late (Tebhaga); caste and communal splits; Congress restraint towards landlords.
- **Historians:** **Bipan Chandra** — integration, class adjustment · **Ranajit Guha**, *Elementary Aspects of Peasant Insurgency* (**1983**) — autonomy · **Shahid Amin**, "Gandhi as Mahatma" (**1984**) · **D. N. Dhanagare** (**1983**) · Majid Siddiqi, Kapil Kumar (Awadh).

> **Line:** "Bardoli fought the state; Eka fought the landlord."

---

## 6. Workers — to 1918
- **Sasipada Banerjee** — Working Men's Club **1870**; *Bharat Shramjeebi* 1874.
- **N. M. Lokhande** — 1884 memorial; **Bombay Mill Hands' Association 1890**; *Deenbandhu*.
- **Early nationalists** opposed Factory Acts **1881, 1891** (Lancashire motive) but backed **Assam tea labour** (Dwarkanath Ganguly).
- **Swadeshi strikes:** Bengal Government Press and railways (Ashwini Coomar Banerjee, Prabhat Kumar Roychowdhury, Premtosh Bose) · **Tuticorin Coral Mills 1908** (**V. O. Chidambaram Pillai**, Subramania Siva) · **Bombay 6-day strike 1908** against Tilak's conviction — **Lenin**: first political strike.

## 7. Workers — 1918–1929
- **Ahmedabad 1918** — Gandhi's fast; **35%** award; **Anasuya Sarabhai** → **ATLA 1920** (trusteeship).
- **Madras Labour Union 1918** — **B. P. Wadia**.
- **AITUC — 31 October 1920, Bombay** — president **Lala Lajpat Rai**, general secretary **Dewan Chaman Lal**; N. M. Joshi · Congress Gaya 1922 welcomed it.
- **Trade Unions Act 1926** · **Trade Disputes Act 1929** · Public Safety Bill 1928.
- **Workers and Peasants Parties 1926–28** · **Girni Kamgar Union** — 6-month Bombay textile strike **1928**.
- **Meerut Conspiracy Case 1929** — ~31 leaders: Muzaffar Ahmed, S. A. Dange, Shaukat Usmani, Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley.
- **Royal Commission on Labour (Whitley) 1929–31**.

## 8. Workers — 1929–1947
- **Splits:** Nagpur **1929** — N. M. Joshi's moderates leave (Indian Trade Union Federation) · **1931** Red TUC · reunited by 1940.
- **1930–32:** Comintern "class against class" → labour **weak in CDM**.
- **1938:** Bombay Trade Disputes Act — unions angered.
- **1942:** CPI "People's War" → out of Quit India; yet Tata Steel and Ahmedabad (3+ months) struck.
- **1945–46:** strike wave · Bombay strike for naval ratings · P&T strike 1946.
- **Centres:** **INTUC May 1947** (Congress) · **HMS 1948** (socialists: Ashok Mehta, T. S. Ramanujam, G. G. Mehta) · **UTUC 1949**.

## 9. Why labour stayed weak — six reasons
1. Small workforce.
2. Rural roots, circular migration.
3. **Jobbers** (*sardars*) controlled hiring.
4. Caste, region, language, religion.
5. Repression — Meerut, Public Safety, Trade Disputes Act.
6. Splits and Comintern swings; Congress ties to Indian capital; Gandhian trusteeship.

**Achievements:** TU Act 1926 · factory laws · political working class in Bombay, Calcutta, Kanpur, Madras.
**Historians:** **Rajnarayan Chandavarkar** (**1994**) — jobbers, neighbourhood · **Dipesh Chakrabarty** (**1989**) — community over class · **Sukomal Sen** (**1977**).

> **Line:** "Indian labour was militant early but weak throughout — small, rural-rooted, divided and split."

## The traps
1. Tebhaga = landlord's share to **one-third**, not zamindari abolition.
2. **Nehru** not a founder of the UP Kisan Sabha (1918).
3. Eka = **rents** (landlord); Bardoli = **land revenue** (state).
4. ATLA founded by **Gandhi**, not Patel or N. M. Joshi.
5. Unions legalised by the **Trade Unions Act 1926**, not the 1881 Factories Act.
6. HMS **socialist** (1948); INTUC **Congress** (1947).
7. Ahmedabad mill strike: **Morarji Desai** was not involved.
