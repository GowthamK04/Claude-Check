## 2. Amendment and basic structure

**Procedure (Art. 368)**
- Introduced in either House, by a minister or a private member, with no prior recommendation.
- Each House passes it separately by special majority: a majority of total membership plus two-thirds of those present and voting.
- Federal provisions also need ratification by half the states, by simple majority, with no time limit.
- There is no joint sitting.
- The President **must** assent (24th Amendment, 1971).

**Changes by simple majority, outside Art. 368:** new states (Arts. 3–4), Legislative Councils (169), the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, quorum, salaries, citizenship.

**Changes that need state ratification:**
- election of the President;
- Union–state powers and the Seventh Schedule;
- representation of states in Parliament;
- the Supreme Court and High Courts;
- the GST Council;
- Art. 368 itself.

**The ladder:**
- Shankari Prasad 1951 and Sajjan Singh 1965: Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights.
- **Golaknath 1967:** it cannot; this case first used prospective overruling.
- **24th Amendment 1971:** reversed Golaknath.
- **Kesavananda 1973:** basic structure.
- **Raj Narain 1975:** free and fair elections are part of it.
- 42nd Amendment 1976: added 368(4)–(5).
- **Minerva 1980:** struck down those clauses.
- **Waman Rao 1981:** Ninth Schedule laws added after 24 April 1973 can be reviewed.
- **Coelho 2007:** tested them against the basic structure.
- **NJAC 2015:** struck down the 99th Amendment.

## 3. Parliamentary system, PM and Council of Ministers

**Art. 75**
- 75(1A): the Council is capped at **15% of the Lok Sabha** (91st Amendment).
- 75(1B): a defector cannot become a minister until re-elected.
- 75(2): ministers serve at the President’s pleasure (individual responsibility).
- **75(3): collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha.**
- 75(5): a non-member can be a minister for **6 months**, then must join either House.

**Other points**
- Art. 78: the PM must inform the President of decisions.
- The **Deputy PM** is not in the Constitution.
- The word **‘cabinet’ appears only in Art. 352** (added by the 44th).
- Cabinet committees are extra-constitutional (Transaction of Business Rules). The PM chairs the ACC and the CCS.

## 4. Federal machinery

| Art. | Rule |
|---|---|
| 248 | Residuary powers with Parliament (from Canada) |
| 249 | Rajya Sabha resolution by **2/3 present and voting**, national interest; lasts 1 year, renewable; the law lapses 6 months later |
| 250 | During a national emergency; laws lapse 6 months after it ends |
| 252 | Two or more states ask; **only Parliament** can amend; other states may adopt |
| 253 | Treaties: any subject, no state consent |
| 254 | Repugnancy: Union law prevails, unless the state law was reserved and assented (Parliament can still override) |
| 256–257, 365 | Union directions; non-compliance can lead to Art. 356 |
| 262 | Inter-state water: Parliament may exclude SC jurisdiction (1956 Act) |
| 263 | **Inter-State Council (1990, after Sarkaria), chaired by the PM; constitutional** |
| 301 | Freedom of trade (Part XIII, from Australia); not a Fundamental Right |

**Zonal Councils:** statutory (1956), chaired by the Home Minister.

**Commissions:** Rajamannar 1969 (set up by Tamil Nadu); Sarkaria 1983 and Punchhi 2007 (set up by the Union).

## 5. The three emergencies

| | National (352) | President’s Rule (356) | Financial (360) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | War, external aggression, **armed rebellion** (44th) | Breakdown of the constitutional machinery; or Art. 365 | Threat to financial stability or credit |
| Advice | Cabinet’s **written** advice | Governor’s report or otherwise | — |
| Approval | **1 month, special majority** | **2 months, simple majority** | **2 months, simple majority** |
| Duration | 6 months per approval, no ceiling | 6 months per approval, **max 3 years**; past 1 year needs an emergency + ECI certificate | No maximum |
| Revocation | Lok Sabha simple-majority resolution; 1/10 of LS members can demand a special sitting (14 days) | President | President |
| Rights | 358: Art. 19 suspended (war or aggression only); 359: others by order, **never 20, 21** | — | Salaries, including judges’, may be cut; state Money Bills may be reserved |
| Used | 1962, 1971, 1975 | 100+ times; use fell after **Bommai 1994** (judicial review, floor test, secularism basic) | Never |

- **Lok Sabha term during an emergency (Art. 83(2)):** extended 1 year at a time, ending no later than 6 months after the emergency.
- **Art. 353:** the Union can give directions to any state.

## 6. President and Vice-President

| | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electoral college | **Elected** MPs + **elected** MLAs, including Delhi and Puducherry (70th) | **All MPs**, elected and nominated |
| Removal | Impeachment for **violation of the Constitution**: signed by 1/4 of a House, 14 days’ notice, passed by **2/3 of total membership** of each House; nominated MPs vote, MLAs do not | Rajya Sabha resolution by effective majority, agreed by the Lok Sabha |
| After a vacancy | Full 5 years | **Full 5 years.** C.P. Radhakrishnan was elected on 9 Sep 2025 (452 votes to 300) after Dhankhar resigned on 21 Jul 2025 |
| Disputes | Supreme Court (Art. 71); acts already done stay valid; a vacancy in the electoral college is no ground | Supreme Court |

**President's powers**
- **Art. 72 pardon:** covers court martial, offences under Union law and death sentences.
  - Exercised on advice (Maru Ram 1980, Kehar Singh 1989), with limited judicial review (Epuru Sudhakar 2006).
  - Unlike the US President, India's President can pardon **only after conviction**.
- **Art. 74(1):** the President can return advice **once** for reconsideration (44th). **74(2):** courts cannot inquire into what advice was given.
- **Art. 123 ordinance:** only when Parliament is not in session; lapses 6 weeks after reassembly; can be retrospective; cannot amend the Constitution.
- **Art. 361:** no court answerability; no criminal case during the term; a civil suit needs 2 months’ notice.

## 7. The deferred accountability bodies

| Body | Basis | Selection | Term | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NITI Aayog | Cabinet resolution, 1 Jan 2015 | PM chairs; Governing Council of all CMs | — | Advises; **no fund allocation** |
| NHRC | PHRA 1993 | PM, Speaker, Home Minister, LoP LS, LoP RS, RS Deputy Chairman | **3 yrs / 70** (2019), reappointment allowed | Chair: former CJI **or** SC judge (2019); **1-year bar**; recommendatory |
| CIC | RTI Act 2005 | PM, LoP (LS), Cabinet Minister nominated by PM | **As prescribed: 3 yrs** (2019 Rules) | DPDP Act 2023 rewrote s. 8(1)(j) (in force Nov 2025) |
| CVC | 1964 (Santhanam) → CVC Act 2003 | PM, Home Minister, LoP (LS) | 4 yrs / 65 | Supervises the CBI in corruption cases; no investigating wing |
| CBI | DSPE Act 1946; resolution 1963 | PM, LoP (or largest party leader), CJI or nominee | Minimum 2 yrs; total up to 5 (2021) | **State consent** (s. 6); constitutional courts can still order a probe |
| Lokpal | 2013 Act; in force 2014; set up **2019** | PM, Speaker, LoP, CJI or nominee, eminent jurist | 5 yrs / 70 | 1 + up to 8, at least half judicial; first P.C. Ghose; **A.M. Khanwilkar since Mar 2024**; covers the PM with safeguards |
| NIA | 2008 Act (after 26/11) | — | — | No state consent; can act abroad (2019) |
| NDMA | DM Act 2005 | **PM chairs** | — | State authorities are chaired by CMs |
| NCW / NCM | Acts of 1990 / 1992 | Union | 3 yrs | Statutory; Jains notified a minority in 2014 |
| Law Commission | Executive order | Union | 3 yrs | Advisory |

**Mains frame (D-W-B-H-G-G-R-J)**

- **Definition:** bodies that hold power to account between elections.
- **Basis:** statutes plus Vineet Narain (1997).
- **How they work:** inquiry, supervision, disclosure, investigation.
- **Gaps:** panels dominated by the executive; powers only to recommend; vacancies; the 2019 RTI and 2023 DPDP changes.
- **Reforms:**
  - Write commissioners’ tenure and pay into the statute (Parliament amends the RTI Act), so the Union cannot vary them.
  - Make NHRC findings binding unless rejected with written reasons laid before the legislature (Parliament amends the PHRA).
  - Give the CBI its own statute (Parliament, as the 2nd ARC and the Standing Committee recommended), so consent and oversight are fixed in law.
- **Judgement:** these bodies are only as independent as their statute and selection panel.

**Other constitutional dimensions (skim)**

- **Language:**
  - Art. 343: Hindi in Devanagari, international numerals.
  - Art. 348: English in the SC and HCs.
  - Art. 350A: mother tongue at primary level.
  - Art. 350B: Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities.
  - Eighth Schedule: **22** languages (14 originally). Sindhi added 1967; Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali 1992; Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali 2003. **English is not listed.**
- **Services:**
  - Art. 310: doctrine of pleasure.
  - **Art. 311:** no dismissal by an authority below the appointing one; inquiry required.
  - **Art. 312:** new All-India Service needs a Rajya Sabha resolution by 2/3 present and voting, then a law.
- **Cooperatives:** 97th Amendment (2011) added Art. 19(1)(c), Art. 43B and Part IXB. Part IXB now applies only to multi-state cooperatives (Rajendra Shah, 2021).
- **Special provisions:** 371A Nagaland, 371G Mizoram, 371J Hyderabad–Karnataka (98th, 2012).

## 8. Elections, anti-defection, parties

**Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule)**
- Added by the **52nd Amendment (1985)**. The **91st (2003)** deleted the 1/3 split exception; only a **merger backed by 2/3** of the legislature party now protects members.
- **Grounds for disqualification:**
  - voluntarily giving up membership;
  - defying the whip, unless the party condones it within 15 days;
  - an **independent** joining any party;
  - a **nominated member** joining a party after 6 months.
- **Who decides:** the Speaker or Chairman, acting as a tribunal. The decision is reviewable (**Kihoto 1992**).
  - It should come within **3 months** (Meghachandra 2020; **Padi Kaushik Reddy v Telangana, 31 Jul 2025**). The 2025 ruling also urged Parliament to rethink the Speaker’s role.

**Representation of the People Acts**
- RPA 1950 covers electoral rolls and seats. RPA 1951 covers conduct of elections, disqualification and corrupt practices.
- A sentence of 2 years or more disqualifies from the date of conviction. **Lily Thomas (2013)** struck down s. 8(4), which had given sitting members time to appeal.

**Electoral funding and choice**
- Electoral bonds were struck down in **ADR, Feb 2024** (Art. 19(1)(a)).
- **NOTA** (PUCL 2013) has no effect on the result.

**Parties**
- Registration is under **s. 29A RPA**. The ECI has no general power to deregister (2002).
- Recognition is under the **Symbols Order 1968**. A national party needs one of:
  - state-party recognition in 4 states;
  - 6% of votes in 4 states plus 4 LS seats;
  - 2% of LS seats from 3 states.
- **Pressure groups** (Almond & Powell): institutional, associational, non-associational, anomic.
- **Kothari:** the ‘Congress system’ (1964), a one-party-dominant system.

## 9. Landmark judgments and doctrines

**The Art. 21 arc:** Gopalan 1950 (rights as separate codes) → Cooper 1970 → **Maneka 1978** (fair, just, reasonable) → Francis Coralie 1981 (dignity) → Olga Tellis 1985 (livelihood) → Subhash Kumar 1991 (environment) → **Vishaka 1997** → **Shreya Singhal 2015** (s. 66A struck, 69A upheld) → **Puttaswamy 2017** (9 judges; overruled M.P. Sharma and Kharak Singh) → **Navtej, Joseph Shine, Common Cause 2018** (passive euthanasia, living wills) → **Ranjitsinh 2024** (climate change, Arts. 14 and 21).

**2023–24 constitution benches:**
- Supriyo 2023: no judicial recognition of same-sex marriage.
- **Art. 370 abrogation upheld** (Dec 2023).
- **Electoral bonds** struck down (Feb 2024).
- **Sita Soren** (Mar 2024): no privilege for bribes; overruled Narasimha Rao 1998.
- **Davinder Singh** (Aug 2024): sub-classification of SCs allowed; overruled Chinnaiah 2004.
- **Property Owners Assn.** (Nov 2024): Art. 39(b) does not cover all private property.
- **AMU** (Nov 2024): Azeez Basha overruled; AMU’s own status left to a regular bench.

**Doctrines:**
- Pith and substance: Balsara 1951.
- Colourable legislation: Gajapati Narayan Deo 1953. It tests competence, not motive.
- **Eclipse:** Bhikaji Narain 1955. Applies to **pre-1950 laws only**.
- Severability: R.M.D.C. 1957.
- Harmonious construction.
- Prospective overruling: **Golaknath 1967**.

**Comparisons:**
- **India vs UK:** India has constitutional supremacy; the UK has parliamentary sovereignty.
- **Secularism:** India keeps ‘principled distance’ (Bhargava) and allows reform under Art. 25(2). France practises laïcité. The US keeps a wall of separation.
- **Pardon:** in India it is on advice, after conviction and reviewable. In the US it is personal and can be pre-emptive.
- **Executive:** in India the executive is fused with the legislature (the PM answers to the Lok Sabha). In the US it is separate (fixed term, veto).
