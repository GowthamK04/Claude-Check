## 2. Seven trap families (run these on every statement)

1. **Authority swap**: ask whether the rule comes from an Article, a statute, the Rules, a convention or a case. Zero Hour is a convention (1962). The PAC chair coming from the opposition is a convention (1967). The Deputy Speaker’s election date is fixed under LS Rule 8. The collegium comes from cases (1993, 1998). The CEC panel comes from a statute (2023 Act).
2. **Actor swap**: name the hand that signs.
   - STs are declared by the President, not the Governor.
   - Office-of-profit disqualification is decided by the President on the ECI’s opinion.
   - A High Court judge is transferred by the President after consulting the CJI.
   - An SPSC member is removed by the President.
   - Panchayat elections are run by the SEC, not the ECI.
3. **Absolute words**: ‘only’, ‘all’, ‘always’ invite one exception. Some absolutes are true, so verify before rejecting.
4. **Number swap**: say the office and its number together.
5. **Amendment or date swap**: ask which amendment and which year.
6. **Scope swap**: check citizens vs persons, Lok Sabha vs Rajya Sabha, Union vs state.
7. **Currency trap**: check the 2023–26 box (Section 9).

**Routine:** mark each statement T, F or ? before reading the options → settle the surest one → strike options that contradict it → change an answer only if you can name the reason.

## 3. Bills and the Houses

| | Ordinary | Money (110) | Financial I (117(1)) | Financial II (117(3)) | Amendment (368) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Introduced in | Either | **LS only** | **LS only** | Either | Either |
| President’s recommendation | No (except Art. 3 etc.) | To introduce | To introduce | For consideration | No |
| Rajya Sabha | Amend / reject | **Recommend only, 14 days** | Amend / reject | Amend / reject | Separate special majority |
| Joint sitting | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| President | Assent / withhold / return once | Assent / withhold — **no return** | All three | All three | **Must assent** (24th) |

- The Speaker certifies a Money Bill and the decision is **final** (Art. 110(3)). Rojer Mathew (2019) referred the question of judicial review to a larger bench.
- **Joint sitting (Art. 108):** triggered when a bill is rejected, the Houses disagree on amendments, or 6 months pass. It is chaired by the **Speaker**, then the Deputy Speaker, then the RS Deputy Chairman. The RS Chairman never chairs it. Joint sittings have been held 3 times: 1961 (Dowry Prohibition), 1978 (Banking Service Commission Repeal) and 2002 (POTA).
- **Lapse on Lok Sabha dissolution:** pending in LS → **lapses**; passed by LS and pending in RS → **lapses**; passed by RS and pending in LS → **lapses**.
- **Bills that survive dissolution:** a bill born in RS that is still pending there and was never sent to LS; a bill for which a joint sitting was notified before dissolution; a bill passed by both Houses and awaiting assent; a bill returned by the President.
- **Prorogation never lapses a bill** (Art. 107(3)).
- **Pocket veto:** Zail Singh, Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986.
- **Quorum:** **1/10 of total membership**, including the presiding officer (Art. 100(3)). The maximum gap between sessions is **6 months** (Art. 85).
- **Speaker:**
  - stays in office after dissolution until just before the new House first meets (Art. 94);
  - is removed by an **effective majority** after **14 days’** notice;
  - votes in the first instance on their own removal, but has no casting vote.
- **Deputy Speaker:** there is no constitutional deadline for the election (Art. 93: ‘as soon as may be’).
- **Disqualification (Art. 103):** decided by the President on the ECI’s opinion. Defection cases go to the Speaker or Chairman under the Tenth Schedule.
- **Privileges (Art. 105):** not codified by any law.
- **Devices:**
  - Adjournment motion: LS only, 50 members, carries censure.
  - No-confidence motion: LS only, 50 members, no reasons needed, targets the whole Council of Ministers.
  - Censure motion: reasons required; can target one minister or the whole Council.
  - Starred question: oral answer with supplementaries. Unstarred: written answer. Short-notice: less than 10 days’ notice.
- **Committees:**
  - PAC: **22** (15 LS + 7 RS), elected by PR-STV; ministers barred; chair appointed by the Speaker (from the opposition by convention since 1967).
  - Estimates Committee: **30, all from LS**, the largest committee.
  - COPU: 22 (15 + 7).
  - DRSCs: **24** in all (16 under LS, 8 under RS), each with **31 members** (21 LS + 10 RS).
  - LS Ethics Committee: **ad hoc 2000 → permanent 2015**. Any person may complain, but only through a member.

**GST Council (279A):** Centre **1/3** of the votes, states **2/3**; decisions need **3/4** of weighted votes. Mohit Minerals (2022): its recommendations are persuasive, not binding.

## 5. Courts and justice

- **Art. 124(1):** the SC's strength is set by Parliament by law. It rose from 34 to 38 by Ordinance in May 2026, replaced by an Act in 2026.
- **Art. 127:** ad hoc judges are appointed by the CJI with the President’s consent, after consulting the HC CJ.
- **Art. 128:** retired judges sit at the CJI’s request, with the President’s consent.
- **Art. 138:** Parliament **can** enlarge the SC's jurisdiction.
- **Art. 143:** the SC ‘may’ give advice, and the President is not bound by it.
  - The 2025 reference on assent was made under 143(1), with the opinion given on **20 Nov 2025**.
- **Collegium:**
  - 1981 (S.P. Gupta): executive primacy.
  - **1993:** collegium created.
  - **1998:** CJI + 4.
  - **2015:** NJAC struck down.
- **Writs:** Art. 226 is wider than Art. 32 (‘any other purpose’). Art. 32 covers Fundamental Rights only. Judicial review is not defined in any single Article.
- **PIL:** relaxed locus standi; epistolary jurisdiction.
- **Tribunals:** **323A** (administrative) by Parliament only; **323B** by Parliament or state legislatures. Both were added by the 42nd Amendment. L. Chandra Kumar (1997) placed them under HC review.
- **Art. 227:** HC superintendence excludes armed-forces tribunals.
- **Subordinate courts:** district judges are appointed by the Governor in consultation with the HC (233); other judges via the SPSC and HC (234).
- **Lok Adalat** (LSA Act 1987): its award is a civil decree with no appeal; only compoundable offences can go to it.
- **Art. 39A** free legal aid: a DPSP added by the 42nd Amendment (asked 2017); courts have also read it into Art. 21.

## 6. Governor and states

- **Art. 200 options:** assent · withhold · return (not a Money Bill; if re-passed, the Governor must assent) · reserve for the President.
  - Reservation is **mandatory if the Bill derogates from the HC’s powers**.
  - The Constitution sets **no time limit**.
- **Art. 201:** the President may assent, withhold or direct return. The House must reconsider within **6 months**. The President is **not bound** even if the Bill is passed again.
- **20 Nov 2025 opinion:** courts cannot fix timelines and there is **no deemed assent**, but a Governor cannot sit on a Bill indefinitely. This overturned that part of the April 2025 Tamil Nadu ruling.
- **Art. 213 ordinance:** only when the legislature is not in session; it ceases **6 weeks** after reassembly. Krishna Kumar Singh (2017): re-promulgation is a fraud on the Constitution. Nabam Rebia (2016): the Governor cannot advance a session against the Council of Ministers’ advice.
- **Art. 164(1A):** the state Council of Ministers is at most **15%** of the Assembly, with a minimum of **12**. The Union has the 15% cap only.
- **Legislative Council:**
  - Created or abolished by **Parliament** on an Assembly resolution passed by special majority (169). This is not an Art. 368 amendment.
  - Size: at most **1/3** of the Assembly, at least **40**.
  - Composition: 1/3 elected by MLAs, 1/3 by local bodies, 1/12 by graduates, 1/12 by teachers; 1/6 nominated.
  - Delay on ordinary bills: **3 months + 1 month**. There is **no joint sitting** in states.

- **Committees on local government:** Balwant Rai Mehta (1957, three-tier); Ashok Mehta (1977, two-tier). The first panchayat was set up at Nagaur, Rajasthan, on 2 Oct 1959.
- **Fifth Schedule:** the Governor can bar or modify any Act for a Scheduled Area. The **Tribes Advisory Council** has up to 20 members, ¾ of them ST MLAs. The Governor reports to the President.
- **Sixth Schedule:** Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and **Mizoram only**; Autonomous District Councils.
- **Delhi:**
  - 69th Amendment, **Art. 239AA**. Police, public order and land are outside the Assembly’s powers.
  - GNCTD (2018): the LG is bound by aid and advice except on those three subjects.
  - GNCTD (2023): services went to the elected government, later reversed by the 2023 amendment Act.

## 8. Foundations (Red zone)

**Acts:**
- **1773** Regulating Act: Governor-General of Bengal; Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774).
- 1784 Pitt’s India Act: Board of Control.
- **1833** Charter Act: Governor-General of India.
- 1853 Charter Act: open competition for the civil services.
- **1858** GoI Act: Crown rule; Secretary of State for India.
- **1861** Councils Act: portfolio system; ordinances; legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras.
- 1892 Councils Act: limited indirect election; budget discussion.
- **1909** Councils Act: separate electorate for Muslims.
- **1919** GoI Act: **provincial** dyarchy; bicameral Centre.
- **1935** GoI Act: provincial autonomy; dyarchy at the Centre (never operated); All-India Federation (never formed, because states’ entry was voluntary); Federal Court (1937).
- 1947 Indian Independence Act.

**Constituent Assembly:**
- Members were indirectly elected by provincial assemblies by STV (Cabinet Mission, 1946).
- 9 Dec 1946: first meeting; **Sachchidananda Sinha** was provisional President.
- 11 Dec 1946: Rajendra Prasad elected President.
- 13 Dec 1946: **Objectives Resolution** moved by Nehru (adopted 22 Jan 1947).
- 22 Jul 1947: flag adopted.
- 29 Aug 1947: Drafting Committee set up under Ambedkar.
- **26 Nov 1949:** Constitution adopted; citizenship, election and provisional-Parliament provisions in force.
- 24 Jan 1950: anthem and song adopted.
- **26 Jan 1950:** Constitution in force.
- When the Assembly sat as a legislature, it was chaired by **G.V. Mavalankar**.

**Preamble:**
- Status on 26 Jan 1950: **Sovereign Democratic Republic**. ‘Unity of the Nation’ became ‘unity and integrity’ in 1976.
- Amended **once** (42nd: socialist, secular, integrity). The challenge was dismissed on **25 Nov 2024** (Balram Singh).
- **Berubari 1960:** not part of the Constitution. **Kesavananda 1973:** part. Not justiciable.

**Borrowings:**
- **UK:** parliamentary government, rule of law, single citizenship, writs, bicameralism.
- **USA:** FR, judicial review, independent judiciary, impeachment, removal of judges, VP.
- **Ireland:** DPSP, nominations to the RS, method of electing the President.
- **Canada:** strong Centre, residuary powers, Governors appointed by the Centre, advisory jurisdiction.
- **Australia:** Concurrent List, freedom of trade, joint sitting.
- **Weimar:** emergency suspension of FR.
- **USSR:** Fundamental Duties; social, economic and political justice.
- **France:** republic; liberty, equality, fraternity.
- **South Africa:** amendment procedure, RS elections.
- **Japan:** procedure established by law.

**Territory and citizenship:**
- First Schedule = states and territories.
- **Art. 3:** new states are formed by ordinary law, introduced on the President’s recommendation. The state’s views are sought but are **not binding**. Art. 4 says this is not an Art. 368 amendment.
- **Ceding territory needs Art. 368** (Berubari; 9th Amendment; 100th Amendment for Bangladesh, 2015). **Settling a boundary does not.**
- Citizenship Act 1955: acquired by birth, descent, registration, naturalisation or incorporation; lost by renunciation, termination or deprivation. Art. 11 leaves the subject to Parliament.
- OCI holders cannot vote.

**Fundamental Rights:**

| Group | Articles |
|---|---|
| Citizens only | 15, 16, 19, 29, 30 |
| All persons | 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25–28 |
| Enforceable against private persons | 15(2), 17, 23, 24 |
| Never suspended | 20, 21 (44th) |
| Suspended automatically (358) | 19, on war or external aggression only |

- **Art. 13:** ‘law’ includes ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications and custom. **13(4)** (24th) excludes amendments.
- **Art. 15(6) / 16(6):** EWS, 103rd Amendment, upheld in Janhit Abhiyan (2022). Indra Sawhney (1992) upheld 27% OBC with the creamy layer excluded, struck down the 10% economic quota and set the 50% ceiling.
- **Art. 17:** untouchability is **not defined**; enforced through the PCR Act 1955.
- **Art. 18:** military and academic distinctions are allowed. Bharat Ratna is **not a title** (Balaji Raghavan 1996).
- **Property:** removed from Art. 19 by the 44th; now **Art. 300A**.
- **Art. 20:** double jeopardy protection applies only before courts; 20(1) covers criminal laws only.
- **Art. 21A:** 6–14, 86th Amendment (2002).
- **Art. 22:** detention beyond 3 months needs an Advisory Board; clauses (1)–(2) do not apply to preventive detention.
- **Art. 30:** religious and linguistic minorities only; culture is protected under Art. 29.
- **Art. 341/342:** SC and ST lists are notified by the **President**; only Parliament can change them.
- **Art. 21** says ‘procedure established by law’. Maneka (1978) made it fair, just and reasonable.

**DPSP and Duties:**
- **Art. 37:** not justiciable, yet fundamental in governance. Minerva Mills (1980): harmony and balance between FR and DPSP is a basic feature.
- **Gandhian:** 40, 43, 46, 47, 48 (cows). **Liberal-intellectual:** 44 UCC, 45, 48 (agriculture), 48A, 49, **50** (separation of the judiciary), 51.
- **42nd:** 39(f), 39A, 43A, 48A. **44th:** 38(2). **86th:** new Art. 45. **97th:** 43B (cooperatives).
- **Duties (51A):** 42nd Amendment, Swaran Singh Committee recommended **8**, **10** were added; the **11th** came with the 86th. Citizens only. 51A(g) covers the environment.
