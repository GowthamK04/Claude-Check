# G5 — Weather Systems: Cyclones, Cloudbursts and ENSO
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G5 · everything here is to be memorised

> **Three generative ideas.** (1) **Latent-heat engine:** warm sea → evaporation → rising air condenses → heat released → pressure falls → more inflow (loop). Cyclones, thunderstorms and cloudbursts are this engine at three sizes. (2) **Spin needs Coriolis** (G4 §5.2): none near the equator; anticlockwise in the NH. (3) **Rain follows warm water:** trades pile warm water; shift the water and the rain moves → ENSO, IOD.
>
> **Spine:** warm sea *because* fuel → rising, condensing air *because* latent heat → low pressure *because* lighter column → inflow *because* high-to-low flow → spin *because* Coriolis + skater effect → self-feeding loop *until* fuel cut at landfall → same engine, smaller, *so* thunderstorms and cloudbursts → rain follows warm water *so* ENSO and IOD steer the monsoon.

## 2. Cyclone basics and the engine *(generative)*
- **Cyclone** = any low with inward-spiralling winds; NH anticlockwise, SH clockwise. **Anticyclone** = high, sinking air, clear weather.
- **Tropical vs temperate:** latent heat vs air-mass contrast · 5–30° vs 35–65° · no fronts vs cold + warm fronts · warm core + eye vs cold core, no eye · ~500–1,000 km, violent vs up to 2,000+ km, moderate · E→W then poleward vs W→E · dies over land vs crosses continents.
- **Ingredients (derive from the engine):** sea **≥ 26.5 °C to ~50 m** (fuel) · **≥ ~5°** latitude (Coriolis) · weak vertical wind shear (upright chimney) · moist mid-air · seed disturbance (easterly wave) · outflow aloft (exhaust).
- **Analogies:** fireplace + chimney; spinning skater (winds fastest near the centre).
- **Structure:** rainbands → **eyewall** (strongest winds, heaviest rain) → **eye** (20–50 km; sinking air → calm, clear, **warmer**; only intense storms) · outflow 12–15 km · **storm surge** (wind + low pressure; main coastal killer; ≠ tsunami).
- **Ends:** landfall (fuel cut + friction) · cold water · shear · dry air. Remnants + Western Disturbance → inland floods (Arnab 2026).
- **Wrong models:** eye most dangerous ✗ · eye colder ✗ · all cyclones have eyes ✗.

## 3. Where and when *(derived)*
- **Names:** hurricane (Atlantic, NE Pacific) · typhoon (NW Pacific, S China Sea) · cyclone (Indian Ocean, Australia) · willy-willy (NW Australia).
- **Western sides of oceans:** trades pile warm water there.
- **None in the S Atlantic and SE Pacific:** cold Benguela/Humboldt, strong shear, ITCZ rarely there.
- **Bay of Bengal ≈ 4× Arabian Sea:** warmer + river-fed freshwater **barrier layer** · Pacific typhoon remnants · AS cooler, drier, sheared. **Deadlier:** shallow funnel-shaped head amplifies surge; low, dense delta (Bhola 1970).
- **NIO seasons:** **Apr–Jun and Oct–Dec**; mid-monsoon shear (upper easterly jet) suppresses them.
- **2014 derivation:** engine needs → all three seas at 10–25° on warm western/northern ocean sides → boosters (W Pacific warm pool; barrier layer + Pacific remnants; Loop Current + African easterly waves) → contrast with S Atlantic → "largely", not only.

## 4. Warming seas *(GS-I 2024)*
- Stronger peaks · **rapid intensification** (Arnab 2026 over >28 °C, barrier layer) · **~7% more moisture per °C** → heavier rain · slower tracks · higher surges · new areas and months.
- **Arabian Sea** warming fastest: more, longer, stronger severe cyclones since 2000 (IITM) — Ockhi 2017, Tauktae 2021, Biparjoy 2023.
- Global **frequency not clearly up; intensity up** ("more cyclones" = wrong model).

## 5. Fujiwhara effect *(GS-I 2026)*
- Sakuhei Fujiwhara, **1921**. Two cyclones **< ~1,400 km** orbit a common centre **nearer the larger storm**, anticlockwise in NH.
- **Movement:** tracks bend, loop, stall; smaller storm flung away. **Intensity:** larger absorbs smaller (merger); outflow shears and weakens the other.
- Examples: **Parma & Melor (2009)** · **Hilary & Irwin (2017)**. Analogy: two skaters holding hands.

## 6. Naming and warnings *(leaf)*
- **IMD ladder (km/h):** depression 31–49 · deep depression 50–61 · **cyclonic storm 62–88 — named** · severe 89–117 · very severe 118–166 · extremely severe 167–221 · **super ≥ 222**.
- **RSMC New Delhi** names from the **WMO/ESCAP Panel** list: **13 countries** (Bangladesh, India, Iran, Maldives, Myanmar, Oman, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Sri Lanka, Thailand, UAE, Yemen); **169 names (2020)**; each used once; Pacific crossers keep their name.
- **Why names:** clarity in warnings, two storms at once, media, public memory.
- **Colours = actions:** green none · yellow be aware · orange be prepared · red take action. **Cyclone stages:** pre-cyclone watch 72 h → alert 48 h → warning 24 h → post-landfall outlook.
- **Names to know:** Phailin 2013 · Fani 2019 · Amphan 2020 · Tauktae 2021 · Biparjoy 2023 · Shakhti, Montha, Senyar, Ditwah 2025 · **Arnab** (Bangladesh; VSCS; near Yanam, 28 Oct 2026) · **Murasu** (SCS; near Ongole, 3 Nov 2026).
- **Odisha model:** 1999 super cyclone ~10,000 dead → Phailin 2013 (~1 million evacuated), Fani 2019 (>1 million); shelters (NCRMP), volunteers, Doppler radars.

## 7. Temperate cyclones *(generative)*
- **Polar-front theory** (Vilhelm & Jacob Bjerknes, Bergen, c. 1918–22).
- Stages: stationary front → wave → mature low (cold + warm front, warm sector) → **occluded**.
- Fuel = air-mass contrast → survives over land; strongest in winter; W→E with the polar-front jet.
- Passage: cirrus and drizzle (warm front) → mild humid sector → heavy showers, cold, clearing (cold front).
- **India: Western Disturbances** — Mediterranean lows on the subtropical jet; winter rain/snow for wheat; with the monsoon → extreme rain (Kedarnath 2013).

## 8. Thunderstorms *(same engine)*
- Moisture + instability + lift. Cumulus (updraft) → mature (up + down; rain, hail, lightning) → dissipating (downdrafts choke inflow).
- **Thunder** = shock wave of lightning-heated air (~25,000 °C), not clouds colliding.
- India: **Nor'westers / kalbaisakhi**; lightning deaths.

## 9. Tornadoes *(GS-I 2024)*
- Shear rolls air horizontally → updraft tilts it upright → **mesocyclone** → funnel.
- **Gulf of Mexico / Tornado Alley:** warm, moist Gulf air (surface) · dry desert air lid (mid-level) · cold, dry Canadian air · jet-stream shear · N–S Rockies + flat plains = no E–W barrier (funnel, G4 §5.6).
- USA **~1,200 a year** (most on Earth); spring peak; **EF0–EF5**.

## 10. Cloudbursts *(GS-I 2024, GS-III 2022)*
- **IMD: ≥ 100 mm in one hour over ~20–30 km².**
- Mechanism: monsoon moisture · steep orographic lift · strong updraft holds the load · collapse → all at once · booster: Western Disturbance.
- Deadly because: narrow V-valleys, loose debris → debris flows, settlements on terraces and fans.
- Events: **Kedarnath 2013** · **Dharali, Uttarkashi, 5 Aug 2025** · **Chasoti, Kishtwar, 14 Aug 2025 (~65 dead, Machail Mata yatra)** — 2025 causes **disputed** (gauges recorded little rain; glacier/GLOF hypothesis).
- Management: Doppler radars, nowcasting, high-altitude automatic gauges, no building on terraces, regulated yatra camps.
- **Wrong model:** every flash flood = cloudburst.

## 11. ENSO *(GS-I 2014; generative)*
- **Normal:** strong trades → warm pool W (Indonesia) → Walker circulation (rise W, sink E) → Peru upwelling, rich fishery.
- **El Niño** ("Christ child"): trades weaken → warm water E → rain shifts E; Peru floods + fishery collapse; Indonesia/Australia drought; **India: weaker monsoon (often)**.
- **La Niña:** stronger trades, cooler E Pacific; wetter Australia/Indonesia; often good Indian monsoon.
- **Southern Oscillation:** Tahiti–Darwin pressure seesaw — **Gilbert Walker, 1920s**; ocean–air feedback (Bjerknes) → "ENSO".
- **Index:** Niño 3.4, **± 0.5 °C**; NOAA's **relative index (RONI) from 2026** removes general warming.
- **2026:** strong El Niño (Niño 3.4 ~ **+2.5 °C**, Nov); India's monsoon **87% of LPA** (deficient); NE India lowest since 1901.
- **Not everything:** 1997 strong El Niño + normal monsoon (positive IOD). El Niño-weak monsoons: 2002, 2009, 2015, 2023, 2026.
- Analogy: blowing across a bathtub; stop and the water sloshes back.

## 12. IOD and MJO
- **IOD** = SST difference **western** tropical Indian Ocean (Arabian Sea/Africa) vs **eastern** (off Sumatra/Java) — *not Pacific*.
- **Positive IOD** → more rain over India and East Africa; can offset El Niño (1997, 2019). **Negative** → can weaken the monsoon. Peaks in autumn.
- **MJO** — eastward-moving band of cloud and rain, **30–60 days** → active/break spells; can trigger cyclones.
