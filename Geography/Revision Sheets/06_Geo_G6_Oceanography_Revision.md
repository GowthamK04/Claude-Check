# G6 — Oceanography
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G6 · everything here is to be memorised

> **Three generative ideas.** (1) **Wind + Coriolis + continents → gyres:** warm currents poleward on **east** coasts, cold equatorward on **west** coasts. (2) **Density (cold + salty = heavy):** thermocline, salinity effects, upwelling, the deep conveyor. (3) **Nutrients rise where water mixes:** fishing grounds; too many nutrients from land → dead zones.
>
> **Spine:** Sun heats the top *so* warm, light water floats on cold → salinity varies *because* evaporation/ice remove water and rain/rivers/melt add it → winds drive currents *because* drag + Coriolis + coasts make gyres → warm east coasts, cold west coasts *because* gyres run poleward on the west side of each basin → cold salty polar water sinks *so* a ~1,000-year conveyor → mixing and upwelling lift nutrients *so* fish (and excess nutrients *so* dead zones) → warming *so* rising seas, bleaching, weaker mixing.

## 2. Ocean and floor
- Oceans **71%** of surface (land:water ≈ 29:71); **Pacific** largest/deepest, ~half of all ocean water; average depth ~3.7 km; **Challenger Deep (Mariana) ~11 km**.
- **Shelf** (≤200 m; fishing, oil, legal shelf) → **slope** (canyons, landslides) → **rise** → **abyssal plain** (3–6 km; nodules) · **mid-ocean ridge** (spreading) · **trench at edges** (subduction) · seamount/guyot.
- Trenches: Mariana · Aleutian (N Pac) · Kermadec, Tonga (S Pac) · Sunda/Java, **Diamantina** (Indian) · S Sandwich, Puerto Rico (Atlantic).
- Wrong model: trenches mid-ocean ✗ — ridges are mid-ocean.

## 3. Temperature *(generative)*
- **Mixed layer** 0–~200 m → **thermocline** ~200–1,000 m (density barrier) → **deep** 0–4 °C.
- **Poles: no thermocline** (cold top to bottom). NH oceans slightly warmer. Warm currents push isotherms poleward. Enclosed tropical seas (Red Sea, Persian Gulf) hottest.
- **OMT** = mean temperature down to the 26 °C isotherm; better monsoon predictor than SST.
- Analogy: unstirred tea with cold milk at the bottom.

## 4. Salinity and density *(generative; GS-I 2017)*
- Average **35‰**. Raise: evaporation, sea-ice formation, enclosed seas, little river inflow. Lower: rain, rivers, melt.
- **Subtropics highest (~36–37‰)** (dry highs) · equator dip (rain) · poles lowest (melt).
- **Red Sea ~40‰+** · Persian Gulf high · **Dead Sea ~340‰** · Lake Van high · **Baltic ~7–8‰** · **Bay of Bengal < Arabian Sea**.
- **Density** rises with cold + salt.
- **Effects:** thermohaline/AMOC → climate · marine life (corals, estuaries, mangroves) · freezing point ~−1.9 °C · salt pans, desalination · ship buoyancy (load lines) · BoB barrier layer → cyclones (G5).
- Wrong model: saltiest at equator ✗.

## 5. Surface currents *(generative)*
- **Forces:** winds (main) · Coriolis · density · gravity/sea-surface slope · coast & floor shape — **not revolution**.
- **Gyres:** clockwise N, anticlockwise S.
- **Warm (east coasts, poleward):** Gulf Stream → N Atlantic Drift · Kuroshio · Brazil · Agulhas · East Australian · Guinea.
- **Cold (west coasts, equatorward):** Canary · California · Humboldt/Peru · Benguela · West Australian. **Polar cold:** Labrador · Oyashio · Falkland.
- **Western intensification** — Gulf Stream, Kuroshio narrow and fast (Stommel 1948).
- **Equatorial counter-current flows east** (convergence/return through doldrums).
- **West Wind Drift** — only current circling the globe (Roaring Forties).
- **Indian Ocean reverses with monsoon**: summer SW monsoon drift eastward/clockwise; winter NE drift westward; Somali current reverses → summer upwelling off Somalia/Oman.
- Visual: NASA "Perpetual Ocean" (3 min): https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3827 · "Perpetual Ocean 2": https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5425

## 6. Deep circulation *(generative)*
- **Thermohaline** = thermo (heat) + haline (salt). Sinks in **Greenland/Labrador Seas (NADW)** and **Weddell Sea (AABW)** — coldest + sea-ice brine. Creeps along floor, rises in Indian/Pacific, returns at surface. **~1,000 years** per loop; slow but huge heat transport.
- **Water masses** = bodies with a set temperature/salinity fingerprint (AABW, NADW, Mediterranean outflow); persist for centuries.
- **AMOC** — Atlantic limb (incl. Gulf Stream); keeps NW Europe warm; Greenland/Arctic meltwater may weaken it (debated: "studies warn").
- Carries heat poleward, stores CO₂, returns nutrients. NOAA explainer: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/amoc.html

## 7. What currents do *(GS-I 2015, 2019, 2022)*
- **Climate:** warm currents warm and moisten coasts (Norway ice-free; Kuroshio–Japan); cold currents → inversion, fog, coastal deserts (Atacama, Namib); fog where warm meets cold (Newfoundland); poleward heat transport.
- **Fishing:** (a) warm–cold convergence — **Grand Banks** (Labrador × Gulf Stream), **NW Pacific** (Oyashio × Kuroshio), **North Sea/Dogger Bank**; (b) **upwelling** — Peru (Humboldt), Namibia (Benguela), California, Canary, Somalia; (c) wide shelves. Chain: nutrients → phytoplankton → zooplankton → fish.
- **Upwelling:** alongshore equatorward wind → **Ekman transport** (Ekman 1905) offshore → cold nutrient-rich water rises. El Niño stops it (Peru anchovy collapse 1972–73).
- **Navigation:** ride currents to save fuel; icebergs and fog from cold currents (*Titanic*, Labrador Current, 1912); ice-free ports (Murmansk, Narvik).
- **Currents vs water masses (2019):** currents move (disperse larvae, carry heat, plastics, oil); water masses persist (set temperature, salinity, oxygen of layers; distinct communities; natural oxygen-minimum zones).
- **Grand Banks hook:** cod collapse → moratorium **2 July 1992**, ~30,000 jobs lost.

## 8. Tides
- Causes: **Moon (≈2× Sun) + Sun + centrifugal force**; two bulges → two highs, two lows a day (~12 h 25 min apart).
- **Spring** (new & full moon; aligned): big range. **Neap** (first & last quarter; right angles): small range. Not at new moon. "Spring" ≠ season.
- High range in bays **wide at mouth, narrow at head** — **Bay of Fundy ~16 m**; **Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kachchh** (India's tidal-energy sites). Tidal bore: Hooghly. Tidal ports: **Kolkata–Haldia, Kandla**. Uses: navigation, silt flushing, fishing, energy.

## 9. Coral reefs *(GS-I 2019)*
- Polyps + symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae). Needs: **20–29 °C**, clear shallow sunlit water (<~50 m), normal salinity, **no silt** (none off Ganga mouth).
- **Fringing** (on shore) · **barrier** (lagoon; Great Barrier Reef ~2,300 km) · **atoll** (ring). **Darwin 1842 subsidence**; Daly glacial-control.
- Value: ~25% of marine species on <1% of sea floor; coastal protection; fisheries; tourism; medicines.
- **Warming:** bleaching (heat ~1 °C above usual max for weeks → algae expelled) · acidification (weaker skeletons) · stronger storms · disease · less recovery time.
- **Global events:** 1998 · 2010 · 2014–17 (68% of reef area) · **2023–mid-2025 (~84%, largest; NOAA)**.
- India: **Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh, Lakshadweep (atolls), Andaman–Nicobar**.
- Wrong model: bleached = dead ✗.

## 10. Dead zones *(GS-I 2018)*
- **Eutrophication** (N, P from fertiliser, sewage) → algal bloom → decay by bacteria consumes O₂ → **hypoxia (< 2 mg/L)**; warm, stratified water stops mixing.
- Where: **Gulf of Mexico** (Mississippi), Baltic, Chesapeake, East China Sea; Arabian Sea & BoB oxygen-minimum zones expanding.
- Consequences: fish kills/migration · bottom life dies · simpler food webs (jellyfish) · fisheries and tourism losses · H₂S and N₂O release · warming feedback.
- Fixes: precise fertiliser (soil-health cards, neem-coated urea), wetlands/buffer strips, sewage treatment, emission cuts.
- Wrong model: caused by toxins ✗ — by nutrients.

## 11. Sea-level rise and islands *(GS-I 2025)*
- Causes: **thermal expansion + land-ice melt** (not floating sea ice). ~**20 cm since 1901**, now ~**4 mm/yr**; 2100: ~0.3–1 m (more if ice sheets destabilise).
- Impacts: land loss (Tuvalu, Kiribati, Marshall Is., Maldives mostly <2 m; **UNDP: 95% of Tuvalu flooded by 2100**) · saltwater into freshwater lens · king tides, surges · coral loss · migration, culture · EEZ and statehood.
- Responses: sea walls; raised islands (**Hulhumalé**); **Kiribati bought land in Fiji (2014)**; **Australia–Tuvalu Falepili Union (in force 2024)** — recognises continuing statehood + migration pathway; **ICJ advisory opinion, 23 July 2025** — no duty to redraw baselines; loss of territory need not end statehood; loss-and-damage funds.
- India: Lakshadweep, Sundarbans, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata; coastal saltwater intrusion.

## 12. Resources and UNCLOS *(GS-I 2014; Prelims 2022)*
- Biotic: fisheries, aquaculture, seaweed, biotech. Abiotic: **offshore oil & gas (~⅓ of world oil)**; **polymetallic nodules** (India ISA area ~75,000 km², Central Indian Ocean Basin); sulphides; placers (monazite, ilmenite); salt. Energy: tidal, wave, offshore wind, **OTEC**. Desalination.
- **Deep Ocean Mission (2021)** — Samudrayaan, **Matsya-6000**, deep-sea mining tech.
- Critique: cost, technology; deep-sea mining ecology (ISA rules unsettled); **over a third of fish stocks overfished (FAO)**; pollution; disputes (South China Sea).
- **UNCLOS 1982:** territorial sea **≤12 nm** (innocent passage) · contiguous **≤24 nm** · **EEZ ≤200 nm** · shelf **≤350 nm**. **India EEZ ≈ 2.02 million km²**. 1 nm = 1.852 km.
