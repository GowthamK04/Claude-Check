# G1 — Earth's Interior, Rocks and Plate Tectonics
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G1 · everything here is to be memorised

> **The spine.** We infer the interior from seismic waves → the Earth is layered (crust, mantle, liquid outer core, solid inner core) → a rigid **lithosphere** rides on a weak **asthenosphere** → the lithosphere is broken into **plates** → plates diverge, converge or slide past → oceans open and close in **Wilson cycles**.

## 2. How we know the interior

- **Direct:** mining and drilling (Kola borehole ~12 km; ocean drilling projects); volcanic material.
- **Indirect:** temperature, pressure and density rise with depth; meteorites; gravity anomalies; the magnetic field; **seismic waves (the main source)**.

## 3. Layers

| Layer | Depth | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Crust | oceanic ~5 km · continental ~30 km · ~70 km under the Himalaya | Continental = granitic (**sial**), lighter, old · oceanic = basaltic (**sima**), denser, young |
| Mantle | Moho to ~2,900 km | Solid but flows slowly · **asthenosphere** in the upper mantle (to ~400 km), weak, partly molten, source of magma |
| Outer core | ~2,900–5,150 km | **Liquid** iron–nickel → generates the magnetic field |
| Inner core | ~5,150–6,371 km | **Solid** iron–nickel; ~13 g/cm³ at the centre |

- **Lithosphere** = crust + rigid top of the mantle, **~10–200 km**. It is the lithosphere, not the crust, that forms plates.
- **Discontinuities:** **Conrad** (inside continental crust) · **Moho** (crust | mantle) · **Repetti** (upper | lower mantle, ~700 km; coaching name) · **Gutenberg** (mantle | core, ~2,900 km) · **Lehmann** (outer | inner core, ~5,150 km).
- **Radius of the Earth:** 6,371 km.

## 4. Seismic waves

| Wave | Particle motion | Travels through | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | along the direction of travel (longitudinal) | solids, liquids, gases | fastest, first |
| S | at right angles (transverse) | **solids only** | second |
| Surface (Love, Rayleigh) | along the surface | surface layers | slowest, last, **most destructive** |

- **S-wave shadow:** S-waves are absent **beyond 105°** from the epicentre → **outer core is liquid**.
- **P-wave shadow:** **105°–145°**; beyond 145° P-waves arrive, refracted through the core.

## 5. Rocks

- **Crust by weight:** O 46.6% · Si 27.7% · Al 8.1% · Fe 5.0% · Ca 3.6% · Na 2.8% · K 2.6% · Mg 2.1%. Feldspar is the most common mineral group, then quartz.
- **Igneous (primary)** — magma or lava cools.
  - **Intrusive / plutonic:** slow cooling, coarse crystals — granite, gabbro.
  - **Extrusive / volcanic:** fast cooling, fine or glassy — basalt, pumice, obsidian.
  - **Acid** (silica-rich, light: granite) vs **basic** (silica-poor, dark: basalt).
  - No layers, no fossils; host metallic ores. India: **Deccan Traps (basalt)**, Rajmahal Traps, Peninsular granites.
  - **Intrusive bodies:** batholith (huge) · laccolith (dome) · lopolith (saucer) · phacolith (wavy) · sill (horizontal) · dyke (vertical).
  - Deccan basalt weathers into **black cotton soil**.
- **Sedimentary** — layers (strata), fossils, often porous; host coal, oil and gas.
  - Mechanical (sandstone, shale, conglomerate, loess) · organic (limestone, chalk, coal) · chemical (rock salt, gypsum).
  - India: **Vindhyan sandstone** (Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri), **Gondwana coal** (Damodar).
  - Covers most of the land surface but is a small share of the crust's volume.
- **Metamorphic** — heat, pressure or fluids, without melting.
  - Contact vs regional. Foliated (slate, schist, gneiss) vs non-foliated (marble, quartzite).
  - Pairs: **limestone → marble · sandstone → quartzite · shale → slate → phyllite → schist · granite → gneiss · coal → graphite**.
  - India: **Makrana marble**, Dharwar schists.
- **Rock cycle:** magma → igneous → (weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction) → sedimentary → (heat, pressure) → metamorphic → (melting) → magma. Any rock can become any other.
- **Porosity ≠ permeability:** clay is porous but impermeable; chalk and sandstone are permeable.

## 6. From drift to plate tectonics

**Continental drift — Wegener, 1912–15**
- **Pangaea** in one ocean, **Panthalassa**; it began splitting about 200 Ma into **Laurasia** (north) and **Gondwanaland** (south).
- **Five evidences:**
  1. Jigsaw fit (Bullard's 1964 computer fit at the 1,000-fathom line).
  2. Same-age rocks: 2,000-million-year belts in Brazil match West Africa.
  3. Tillite: India's Gondwana sediments have counterparts in six southern landmasses.
  4. Placers: Ghana's gold comes from the Brazil plateau.
  5. Fossils: *Mesosaurus* (South Africa, Brazil), *Glossopteris*, lemurs (India, Madagascar, Africa).
- **Proposed forces:** pole-fleeing and tidal → too weak → theory rejected.
- **Holmes (1930s):** mantle convection currents driven by radioactive heat — the missing engine.

**Sea-floor spreading — Hess, 1961–62 (with Dietz)**
- New crust forms at mid-ocean ridges and is destroyed at trenches.
- **Evidence:**
  1. Rocks are youngest at the ridge, older with distance.
  2. Ocean floor is **under 200 Ma**; continents up to ~3,200 Ma.
  3. Sediments are thin near ridges.
  4. **Symmetric magnetic stripes** (Vine & Matthews, 1963).
  5. Quakes are shallow at ridges, deep at trenches.

**Plate tectonics — McKenzie & Parker (1967), Morgan (1968)**
- **Major plates:** Antarctic · North American · South American · Pacific · India–Australia–New Zealand · African · Eurasian.
- **Minor plates:** Cocos, Nazca, Arabian, Philippine Sea, Caribbean, Scotia.
- **Speeds:** <2.5 cm/yr at the Arctic Ridge to >15 cm/yr at the East Pacific Rise. India moves ~4–5 cm/yr north-east.
- **Driving forces:** mantle convection · ridge push · **slab pull** (generally the largest).

## 7. Plate boundaries

| Boundary | Landform | Quakes / volcanoes | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divergent (constructive) | mid-ocean ridge, rift valley | shallow / fissure basalt | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Iceland, East African Rift, Red Sea |
| Ocean–continent | trench + coastal fold range | deep / explosive | Nazca → Andes, Peru–Chile Trench |
| Ocean–ocean | trench + volcanic island arc | deep / arc volcanoes | Mariana Trench, Japan |
| Continent–continent | highest fold mountains, plateau | strong / **almost no volcanoes** | India + Eurasia → Himalaya, Tibet |
| Transform (conservative) | fault, offset ridge | shallow / none | San Andreas |

## 8. Distribution — the 2014 and 2020 questions

- **Young fold mountains are at margins** because convergent boundaries lie along continental edges (Andes, Rockies) and collision sutures (Himalaya, Alps). **Old fold mountains are inland** — Appalachians, Urals, **Aravallis**: ancient sutures, now worn down.
- **Collision gives quakes but almost no volcanoes:** continental crust is too buoyant to subduct and melt.
- **Earthquake belts (USGS):** Circum-Pacific **~81%** of the largest earthquakes · Alpide (Java–Sumatra–Himalaya–Mediterranean–Atlantic) **~17%** · Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
- **Circum-Pacific characteristics:**
  - ~40,000 km horseshoe of subduction margins.
  - Deep trenches (Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, ~11 km).
  - Island arcs (Aleutians, Japan, Philippines); young fold mountains (Andes, Rockies).
  - Most active volcanoes (often given as ~75%).
  - Deep-focus quakes along Wadati–Benioff zones; most tsunamis.
  - Geothermal energy and copper belts.
- **Exceptions:** intraplate quakes (**Latur 1993**) and hotspot volcanoes (**Hawaii**).

## 9. Indonesia and the Philippines — the 2014 question

- About 17,000 islands in Indonesia and about 7,600 in the Philippines.
- **Subduction arcs:** the Indo-Australian plate subducts under the Sunda plate at the **Java (Sunda) Trench** → Sumatra–Java–Bali. The Philippines are squeezed from both sides (Philippine Trench in the east, Manila Trench in the west).
- **Drowned Sunda Shelf:** sea-level rise after the last ice age split Borneo, Sumatra and Java.
- **Coral islands; accreted fragments** (Sulawesi, Banda arc).
- **India link:** the same arc runs north as Andaman–Nicobar → **Barren Island** (India's only active volcano), the 2004 Sumatra–Andaman earthquake and tsunami.

## 10. Hotspots and mantle plumes — the 2018 question

- **Mantle plume:** a narrow upwelling of abnormally hot rock from the deep mantle, possibly the core–mantle boundary. **Hotspot:** its surface volcanism, usually **inside** a plate.
- **Hotspot track:** volcanoes age with distance from a fixed plume → **Hawaii–Emperor chain**; its bend records a change in the Pacific plate's direction.
- **India:**
  - **Réunion plume → Deccan Traps (~66 Ma)**; its track runs Lakshadweep–Maldives–Chagos → Réunion.
  - **Kerguelen plume → Rajmahal Traps and the Ninetyeast Ridge**.
- **Role:**
  1. A fixed reference frame for plate motion.
  2. Starts rifts (Afar).
  3. Builds large igneous provinces.
  4. Minor "plume push".
  5. Caveat — whether every hotspot has a deep plume is debated.

## 11. Changing continents and oceans — the 2025 question

- **Wilson cycle** (J. Tuzo Wilson, 1966):
  1. Embryonic — continental rift (**East African Rift**).
  2. Young — narrow sea (**Red Sea**, Gulf of Aden).
  3. Mature — wide ocean with a ridge (**Atlantic**).
  4. Declining — subduction shrinks it (**Pacific**).
  5. Terminal — remnant sea (**Mediterranean**).
  6. Suture — collision mountains (**Himalaya**, where the Tethys closed).
- **Supercontinent cycle:** Rodinia (~1 billion years ago) → Pangaea (assembled by ~300 Ma, breaking up from ~200 Ma).
- **Other changes:** **accretion** at continental edges (western North America) · **sea-level change** drowns or exposes shelves (Sunda Shelf) · **isostasy** — crust rises after ice melts (Scandinavia).

## 12. India as a moving plate

1. Part of **Gondwanaland** → Gondwana coal; shared fossils with Africa, Antarctica and Australia.
2. Drifted north across the **Tethys** for over 100 million years, unusually fast; palaeomagnetism records the northward movement.
3. Passed over the **Réunion** plume → **Deccan Traps** (~66 Ma).
4. Collision with Eurasia began **~40–50 Ma** → **Himalaya**, still rising, hence highly earthquake-prone.
5. Eastern edge subducts under the Burma plate at the **Andaman–Sunda Trench** → Andaman earthquakes, Barren Island.

## 14. The three lines to have word-perfect

1. S-waves cannot pass through liquid, so their absence beyond 105° proves the outer core is liquid.
2. Wegener supplied the evidence; Hess supplied the mechanism; plate tectonics supplied the framework.
3. Collision builds the highest mountains but not volcanoes, because continental crust does not subduct and melt.

## 15. Confusions to avoid

- Lithosphere ≠ crust · asthenosphere ≠ mantle.
- Clay is porous but impermeable.
- Vosges and the Black Forest are block mountains.
- Barren Island is still active.
- Hotspots are inside plates.
- Ocean floor is always younger than continental rock.
- Conrad and Repetti are coaching names — fine for Prelims; do not lean on them in Mains.
