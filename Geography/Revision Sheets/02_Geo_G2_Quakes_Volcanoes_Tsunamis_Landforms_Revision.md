## 2. Earthquakes

- **Definition:** "Sudden release of strain energy accumulated along a fault, propagated as seismic waves from the focus."
- **Elastic rebound** (H. F. Reid, after San Francisco 1906): locked fault bends → snaps → springs back. Road-across-a-fault sketch: straight → bent → offset.
- **Focus (hypocentre)** = where rupture starts at depth; **epicentre** = surface point vertically above. Shallow (0–70 km) most damaging; intermediate 70–300; deep 300–700 km only in subducting slabs (Wadati–Benioff zone).
- **Waves:** P (push–pull, fastest, all states) · S (shake, solids only) · surface waves L and R (slowest, most destructive).
- **Magnitude vs intensity:** magnitude = energy at source, one value (Richter ML 1935 → moment magnitude **Mw** today, fault area × slip); intensity = shaking/damage at a place, many values (**Modified Mercalli I–XII**); isoseismals join equal intensity. **+1 magnitude = ×10 amplitude, ~×32 energy.**
- **Types:** tectonic · volcanic · collapse · explosion · **reservoir-induced (Koyna 1967, M6.3)**.
- **Belts:** Circum-Pacific (~4/5 of great quakes) · Alpine–Himalayan (collision) · mid-ocean ridges (shallow, moderate) · intraplate (reactivated faults).
- **Effects:** shaking → collapse · **liquefaction** (wet sand turns liquid — Bhuj) · landslides · tsunami · fire · homelessness, disease, economic loss.
- **"Earthquakes don't kill people; buildings do."**

### India — three settings
| Setting | Cause | Events |
|---|---|---|
| Himalaya + NE | Thrusts of India–Eurasia collision (MCT, MBT, **Main Frontal Thrust**, rooted in Main Himalayan Thrust); India moves ~4–5 cm/yr | 1897 Shillong M8.1 · 1905 Kangra M7.8 · 1934 Bihar–Nepal M8.0 · **1950 Assam M8.6 (India's largest)** · 1991 Uttarkashi M6.8 · 1999 Chamoli M6.6 · 2005 Kashmir M7.6 · 2011 Sikkim M6.9 · 2015 Gorkha M7.8 |
| Kutch + Peninsula | Reactivated old faults; dam loading | 1967 Koyna M6.3 · **1993 Latur M6.2 (~10,000 dead, "stable" zone, stone houses at night)** · 1997 Jabalpur M5.8 · **2001 Bhuj M7.7 (26 Jan, ~20,000 dead, liquefaction)** |
| Andaman–Nicobar | Subduction under Burma microplate | **2004 M9.1 megathrust → tsunami** |

- **Central Seismic Gap:** Uttarakhand–western Nepal, between 1905 and Nepal ruptures; no great (M8+) quake since c. 1505 → stored strain.
- **Seismic code in force: IS 1893 (Part 1): 2016 — four zones II, III, IV, V; ~three-fifths (≈59%) of land in III–V.**
- **IS 1893:2025** (probabilistic, PSHA): new **Zone VI** = whole Himalayan arc, ~61% land moderate–high; notified **Nov 2025**; MoHUA objected (cost, consultation); **withdrawn by gazette 3 March 2026**; 2016 code reinstated. *(Evolving — re-check before Mains.)*

## 3. Volcanoes

- **Definition:** "Movement of magma from the interior to the surface and its extrusion as lava, gases and pyroclasts." Magma underground; lava at surface.
- **Three ways to melt:** **flux** (water from slab, subduction → andesite, explosive arcs: Andes, Japan, Barren Is.) · **decompression** (ridges/rifts → basalt: Iceland, East African Rift) · **plume** (hotspot → basalt: Hawaii, Réunion → Deccan).
- **Silica → viscosity → violence.** Basaltic: low silica, ~1,100–1,200 °C, runny, gas escapes, effusive. Rhyolitic/andesitic: high silica, cooler, sticky, gas trapped, explosive.
- **Forms:** shield (Mauna Loa) · composite/strato (Fuji, Vesuvius, Barren Is.) · cinder cone (Parícutin) · caldera (Toba, Crater Lake, Yellowstone) · fissure/flood basalt (Deccan Traps ~66 Ma, Columbia Plateau) · lava dome.
- **Styles (rising violence):** Hawaiian → Strombolian → Vulcanian → Peléan (**nuée ardente**, St Pierre 1902) → Plinian (Vesuvius 79 CE). **VEI 0–8** (Tambora 7).
- **Activity:** active (Barren Is.) · dormant (Narcondam) · extinct.
- **Distribution:** ~3/4 of active volcanoes round the Pacific; Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift, Mediterranean, hotspots. **Himalaya and Alps: almost none** (continents don't subduct deep).
- **Environment (3 scales):** local — lava, pyroclastic flows, **lahars** · regional — ash on crops and water, roof collapse, airspace closure, SO₂ acid rain, new land · global — stratospheric sulphate aerosols cool (**Pinatubo 1991 ~0.5 °C**; **Tambora 1815 → 1816 "Year Without a Summer"**); Hunga Tonga 2022 added water vapour (warming effect debated). **Benefits:** fertile soils (Java; black cotton soil), geothermal (Iceland; **Puga Valley**, Ladakh), minerals, geoparks.
- **2021 eruptions:** **Cumbre Vieja, La Palma** (hotspot; 85 days Sept–Dec; buildings and banana plantations buried; lava delta) · **Nyiragongo** (rift; Goma evacuated) · **La Soufrière, St Vincent** (arc; ash, water contamination) · **Fagradalsfjall, Iceland** (ridge; first on Reykjanes in ~800 yr) · **Semeru, Java** (arc; pyroclastic flows, lahars) · **Etna** (lava fountains, airport closures).
- **India:** **Barren Island** — only active volcano; Andaman Sea; ~140 km NE of Port Blair; erupted 1787, dormant till 1991, minor eruptions **13 and 20 Sept 2025** · **Narcondam** dormant · **Baratang** — India's only active **mud volcanoes** · Deccan and Rajmahal Traps = ancient flood basalts (Réunion, Kerguelen hotspots).
- **Volcano places:** St Helens, Rainier — USA · Etna, Vesuvius, Stromboli — Italy · Parícutin — Mexico · Taal, Pinatubo — Philippines · Krakatoa, Tambora, Semeru — Indonesia · Tungurahua, Cotopaxi — Ecuador · Nyiragongo — DR Congo · Kilimanjaro — Tanzania · Fuji — Japan · Hunga Tonga — Tonga. **No eruptions: Baltic Sea.**

## 4. Tsunamis

- **Definition:** "Series of long-period sea waves caused by sudden vertical displacement of a large volume of ocean water, chiefly by sub-sea megathrust earthquakes." Japanese "harbour wave".
- **Triggers:** megathrust (2004 M9.1; **Kamchatka 29–30 July 2025 M8.8**) · submarine/coastal landslide (**Lituya Bay 1958**) · volcanic collapse/blast (**Krakatoa 1883, Hunga Tonga 2022**) · meteorite. **Strike-slip quakes rarely make tsunamis** (Myanmar 2025).
- **Physics:** speed ≈ √(g × depth) → **700–800 km/h** in 4–5 km deep water; height **< 1 m**; wavelength 100–200+ km; whole water column moves. Shallow water → **shoaling** → 10–30 m. **Drawback** (sea retreats first) = natural warning.
- **Tsunami ≠ storm surge** (surge: cyclone wind + low pressure, local, days of warning — G5).
- **Where:** ~4/5 in Pacific Ring of Fire (Chile, Japan, Alaska, Kamchatka, Indonesia). Indian Ocean: **Sunda–Andaman arc** (east) and **Makran** (west; 1945 tsunami reached Gujarat and Mumbai). Atlantic: Lisbon 1755.
- **2004:** M9.1 off Sumatra; **~2.3 lakh dead, 14 countries; 10,000+ in India** (Tamil Nadu, A&N).
- **Consequences (4 layers):** human · economic (fisheries, ports, tourism) · ecological (salinised soil and wells; corals and mangroves damaged; **mangroves shielded villages — Pichavaram**) · geomorphic (parts of Andamans uplifted, others sank; **Indira Point lighthouse left in the sea**).
- **India since 2004:** **ITEWC at INCOIS, Hyderabad (2007)** — seismic network + tsunami buoys (bottom pressure recorders) + tide gauges; minutes-scale advisories; serves Indian Ocean countries · **NDMA tsunami guidelines 2010**: hazard mapping, warning chain, techno-legal regime and CRZ, shelters and **bio-shields**, awareness, drills, community DM · **UNESCO Tsunami Ready:** Noliasahi and Venkatraipur (2020) + **24 Odisha villages (Nov 2024)**; 12 indicators; renew every 4 years; Odisha has 381 tsunami-prone villages.
- **"The wave is unstoppable; the disaster is not."**

## 5. Risk and governance

- **Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity.**
- **Contrast:** Kamchatka July 2025 M8.8 (top-10 ever, ~4 m waves) → very few deaths (low exposure, fast warnings, evacuations) vs **Myanmar 28 March 2025 M7.7** (~32× less energy; shallow strike-slip **Sagaing Fault**, ~460 km rupture under Mandalay; Bangkok tower collapsed 1,000+ km away) → thousands dead.
- **DM Act 2005** (after 2004 tsunami): NDMA (PM chairs), SDMA, DDMA, NDRF.
- **DM (Amendment) Act 2025** (Act 10 of 2025; assent **29 March 2025**): NDMA and SDMAs prepare plans themselves · national and state disaster databases · statutory **NCMC** (and High-Level Committee) · **Urban Disaster Management Authorities** for state capitals and municipal-corporation cities.
- **Earthquake governance:** National Centre for Seismology (MoES) monitors; **no reliable prediction exists**; BIS IS 1893:2016, IS 13920 (ductile detailing), IS 4326; National Building Code 2016; **NDMA earthquake guidelines 2007**.
- **Gains:** working tsunami warning; legal frame; codes since 1962; Tsunami Ready; Urban DMAs (2025).
- **Gaps:** non-engineered buildings, weak enforcement (hill towns, urban fringes); little microzonation (soft soils — Yamuna floodplain, Guwahati); slow retrofitting; 2025–26 code rollback (cost beat safety); CRZ bent by tourism/ports; response bias.
- **Reforms (mechanism · institution · accountability):**
  - Phased probabilistic code, critical infrastructure first · BIS + MoHUA + state PWDs · no funding/clearance if non-compliant; published structural audits.
  - Permits tied to microzonation · NCS (maps) + state urban depts + Urban DMAs · engineer's licence liable; municipal commissioner answerable to SDMA.
  - Retrofit schools/hospitals in Zones IV–V via NDMF/SDMF · NDMA + state depts · annual report to legislature; CAG audit.
  - Scale Tsunami Ready + mangrove bio-shields + CRZ · INCOIS/NTRB + SDMAs + CZMAs · recognition lapses unless renewed; CRZ violations before NGT.
- **Judgement:** "India has moved from relief to readiness for tsunamis, where one central warning protects all; not yet for earthquakes, where safety depends on millions of buildings — enforcement, not seismology, is the binding constraint."

## 6. Exogenic processes and landforms

- **Endogenic vs exogenic:** internal heat (diastrophism, volcanism) raises relief; solar energy + gravity (weathering, mass wasting, erosion, deposition) levels it = **gradation** (degradation + aggradation).
- **Weathering (in place):** physical (frost wedging, exfoliation, salt) · chemical (**carbonation** — CO₂ + rain = carbonic acid dissolves limestone; **oxidation** — O₂ rusts iron minerals; hydration, hydrolysis) · biological. Hot-wet → chemical (laterite); cold/dry → physical (scree).
- **Weathering prepares; erosion removes; deposition builds.**

| Agent | Erodes by | Erosional forms | Depositional forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| River | hydraulic action, abrasion, solution | V-valley, gorge, canyon, waterfall, rapids, potholes, interlocking spurs, meanders | floodplain, levees, point bars, ox-bow lakes, braided channels, delta (or estuary) |
| Glacier | plucking, abrasion | cirque (+ tarn), arête, horn (Matterhorn), U-valley, hanging valley, fjord | moraine (till: lateral, medial, terminal), drumlin, esker, outwash plain, kettle |
| Wind | deflation, abrasion, attrition | deflation hollow (Qattara), mushroom rock, yardang, ventifact, desert pavement | dunes (barchan, seif, parabolic, transverse), loess (Loess Plateau, China) |
| Groundwater | solution | swallow hole, sinkhole/doline, uvala, polje, caves | stalactite, stalagmite, pillar |
| Waves | hydraulic action, abrasion | cliff, wave-cut notch & platform, cave → arch → stack → stump | beach, spit, bar, lagoon, tombolo |

- **River courses:** upper (vertical erosion) → middle (lateral erosion, meanders) → lower (deposition near base level). **Ox-bow:** outer bank erodes, neck narrows, flood cuts it. Indian cases: Indus/Sutlej gorges, Dhuandhar (Narmada), Gandikota canyon (Pennar), Majuli, Ganga–Brahmaputra delta, Narmada/Tapi estuaries.
- **Kosi:** shifted 100+ km west in ~200 years ("Sorrow of Bihar").
- **Fjord — 4 steps:** (1) Pleistocene glaciers flow down coastal river valleys → (2) plucking + abrasion make U-troughs **over-deepened below sea level**, **sill** at mouth → (3) ice melts, sea rises **100+ m** → (4) sea floods trough. **Isostatic rebound** keeps walls rising. **Deepest inland** (Sognefjord >1 km deep).
- **Why picturesque:** sheer walls from deep calm water; hanging-valley waterfalls; snow peaks mirrored in still water behind the sill; rock–forest–ice–sea contrast. **Geirangerfjord, Nærøyfjord (UNESCO)** · Milford Sound (NZ) · Chile · Greenland · Alaska · British Columbia. **India has no fjords.** Human value: deep harbours, fisheries, aquaculture, hydropower; threats: over-tourism, cruise pollution.
- **Wind transport:** suspension (clay/silt < 0.06 mm, far: dust, loess) · **saltation** (sand hops < 1 m — most transport) · surface creep (coarse grains roll). Abrasion strongest within **~1–2 m** of ground → mushroom rocks.
- **Dunes:** **barchan** crescent, horns point **downwind**, little sand, steady wind (Thar, Sahara) · **seif** long ridges **parallel** to wind · **parabolic** horns point **upwind**, held by plants (coasts, desert margins).
- **In deserts water is the bigger sculptor:** flash floods → wadis, bajadas (merged fans), pediments, inselbergs, playas (Sambhar, Didwana salt lakes).
- **Karst:** limestone + carbonic acid. Stalac**t**ite hangs from **t**op; stalag**m**ite rises as **m**ound. India: Meghalaya caves (Krem Liat Prah, Mawmluh), Borra and Belum (AP), Guptadham (Bihar). **Meghalayan Age** (last 4,200 yr) named in 2018 from a **Mawmluh Cave stalagmite** recording the 2200 BCE mega-drought.
- **Coasts:** emergent (land rose/sea fell — east coast; **Rann of Kutch**) vs submergent (sea drowned land — fjords, rias; parts of west coast). Lagoons behind bars: **Chilika** (Asia's largest brackish lagoon), Pulicat, Vembanad, Ashtamudi. Periyar = reservoir.

## 7. Desertification

- **UNCCD (1994; India ratified 1996):** "land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities." Not deserts expanding. **Land degradation** = loss of productivity anywhere; desertification = its dryland subset.
- **ISRO–SAC Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas (2021, data 2018–19):** **97.85 Mha = 29.7%** of 328.72 Mha degraded (94.53 Mha in 2003–05) · **83.69 Mha desertified** · water erosion ~11% · vegetation degradation ~9% · **wind erosion ~5.5%** of India's area.
- **Commitment:** restore **26 Mha by 2030**; land-degradation neutrality (SDG 15.3).
- **The loop:** human pressure (overgrazing, fuelwood, marginal ploughing, bad irrigation) → plant cover lost → wind + water erosion → soil loses fines and moisture → lower productivity → more pressure. **Drought is the trigger; people are the driver.** Charney (1975): bare soil, higher albedo, less rain.
- **"No climate boundaries" (2020):** (1) human drivers act everywhere · (2) degraded land in every climate — Chambal ravines (semi-arid), **Cherrapunji** and NE hills (humid — wettest place, water-short, degraded), salinised Punjab/Haryana (irrigated), Ladakh (cold desert), Sahel · (3) climate change shifts dry sub-humid zones · **qualify:** drylands most fragile. Close: "a land-use problem expressed through climate."
- **Wind's 5 roles (2026):** (1) **deflation** strips clay, silt, organic matter → coarse infertile sand/pavement · (2) **abrasion** damages seedlings, crops, structures · (3) **dune reactivation + sand encroachment** on fields, canals (Indira Gandhi Canal), roads · (4) **dust storms** (May 2018, Rajasthan–UP, 100+ dead; dust darkens Himalayan snow) · (5) **albedo feedback**. Balance: water erosion ~2× wind nationally; wind dominates W Rajasthan and Gujarat.
- **Saharan dust feeds the Amazon:** ~22,000 t phosphorus a year (NASA satellite estimate).
- **Responses:** shelterbelts, windbreaks, dune fixing (**CAZRI**, Jodhpur), rotational grazing, watersheds (PMKSY), Green India Mission, **Aravalli Green Wall** — launched March 2023, relaunched by PM **5 June 2025**; 5-km buffer; **29 districts** in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana, Delhi; 6.45 Mha buffer, 42% degraded.
- **Reforms:** district LDN targets on the ISRO atlas (MoEFCC + states; funding tied to measured change) · commons to gram sabhas with grazing rules (social audit) · uniform Aravalli definition + no new mining in buffer (MoEFCC + states; NGT).

## 10. Confusions to avoid

- Magnitude ≠ intensity · energy per step ~32×, not 10× or 100×.
- Seismic code in force = **2016, four zones**; Zone VI was withdrawn.
- Barren Island is ~140 km NE of **Port Blair**, not of Great Nicobar; Baratang = mud volcano; Narcondam = dormant.
- Deccan Traps are ancient fissure basalts, not active volcanoes.
- Tsunami ≠ storm surge · not every big quake makes a tsunami (needs vertical sea-floor movement).
- Barchan horns downwind; parabolic horns upwind; seif parallel.
- Desertification ≠ desert expansion; it is dryland degradation.
- Fjords are deepest inland, with a shallow sill at the mouth.
- Periyar is not a lagoon.
