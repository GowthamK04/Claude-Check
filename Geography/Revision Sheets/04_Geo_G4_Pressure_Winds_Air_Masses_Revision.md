## 2. Pressure and belts
- Sea-level pressure ≈ **1013.25 hPa**; falls rapidly with height (~1 hPa per 10 m near ground). Barometer **falling → storm**, rising → fair.
- Warm air expands, rises → **low**; cold air contracts, sinks → **high**; also dynamic piling/sinking.

| Belt | Latitude | Origin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equatorial Low / ITCZ | 0–5° | **thermal** (rising) | doldrums; trades converge |
| Subtropical High | 30–35° | **dynamic** (sinking) | **horse latitudes — HIGH**; deserts |
| Sub-polar Low | 60–65° | **dynamic** (rising) | best over oceans; Aleutian, Icelandic lows |
| Polar High | 90° | **thermal** (cold sinking) | polar easterlies |

- All belts shift **5–10°** north (July) / south (January). ITCZ over India ~**20–25° N** in July (monsoon trough — G9).

## 3. Forces on wind
- **Pressure-gradient force:** high → low; close isobars = strong wind.
- **Coriolis:** **F = 2vΩ sin φ** → grows with speed · **zero at equator, max at poles** · right (NH), left (SH) · changes direction, not speed · same rule both hemispheres. Coriolis 1835; sink-drain story is a myth.
- **Friction:** lowest ~1 km; wind crosses isobars toward low. Aloft: **geostrophic wind** ∥ isobars.
- **NH cyclone (low): anticlockwise, inward, rising, rain · NH anticyclone (high): clockwise, outward, sinking, clear.** SH reversed.
- No tropical cyclones within ~5° of equator (no Coriolis) — G5.

## 4. Planetary winds and cells
- **Trades** 5–30°: NE (N), SE (S); steadiest; converge at ITCZ.
- **Westerlies** 35–60° in **each** hemisphere: SW (N), NW (S); **SH stronger — less land**: Roaring Forties (40°), Furious Fifties, Screaming Sixties.
- **Polar easterlies** from polar highs to sub-polar lows.
- **Hadley** (George Hadley 1735) and **Polar** cells thermally direct; **Ferrel** (William Ferrel 1856) indirect. Rising air (0°, 60°) = rain; sinking (30°, 90°) = dry.
- **Ocean link (2021):** tropical trades pile warm water on **western** sides of oceans; temperate westerlies warm **eastern** sides.

## 5. Jet streams
- Narrow fast westerly bands near tropopause, **9–14 km**, **120–300+ km/h**, on strong temperature gradients.
- **Subtropical jet:** ~25–35°, ~12 km, **winter max**; splits round Tibet; southern branch over N India steers **Western Disturbances**; northward jump → monsoon onset (G9).
- **Polar-front jet:** 40–60°, ~9–10 km; **Rossby waves**; steers mid-latitude cyclones; slower, wavier jet stalls weather (G3).
- **Tropical easterly jet:** ~15° N, summer only, over peninsular India after Tibet heats; linked to monsoon strength.
- Hook: Japanese **Fu-Go** balloons (1944–45) rode the jet to North America; 6 killed in Oregon, May 1945.

## 6. Local winds (2026)
- **Land/sea breeze:** day sea → land; night land → sea (daily mini-monsoon). **Valley/mountain breeze:** day upslope; night downslope (katabatic) → valley inversions.
- **Foehn effect:** rising — **~10 °C/km dry**, then **~5 °C/km saturated** (latent heat; rain windward) → descending dry — **~10 °C/km** → warmer, drier lee (e.g., 20 °C → 30 °C).
- **Foehn** (Alps) · **Chinook** (east of Rockies; "snow-eater"; **Spearfish, 22 Jan 1943: −20 → +7 °C in 2 minutes**) · **Zonda** (east of Andes, Argentina) · **Santa Ana** (S California, downslope desert wind; wildfires).
- **Loo:** hot (45 °C+), dry, dusty W/SW wind, NW Indian plains, **May–June afternoons**; **advected** desert heat to the summer heat-low — **not** a foehn; ends with monsoon.
- **Comparison (Loo / Chinook / Foehn):** region — NW India plains / Great Plains E of Rockies / N Alpine valleys · season — May–June / winter–spring / winter–spring · nature — very hot, dusty / warm, gusty, sudden / warm, dry, clear · harm — heatstroke, crops, water, power, dust / fires, thaw floods, drought / avalanches, fires, "foehn sickness" · help — dries rabi, deepens heat-low / clears snow, grazing, longer season / early melt, ripens grapes and maize.
- Others: **Sirocco** (Sahara → Italy, "blood rain") · **Khamsin** (Egypt) · **Harmattan** (W Africa, dry NE, "the doctor") · **Samun/Simoom** (Arabia, Kurdistan) · **Brickfielder** (Australia) · cold: **Mistral** (Rhône), **Bora** (Adriatic).

## 7. Mountains and local weather (2021)
- **Alignment:** E–W — Atlas, Pyrenees, Alps, Caucasus, Zagros, Hindu Kush, Himalaya · N–S — Rockies, Coast Ranges, Sierra Madre, Andes, Great Dividing Range, Western Ghats, Urals, Appalachians.
- **Five effects:** (1) **barrier** — Himalaya block Siberian cold, hold monsoon; Alps shelter Mediterranean · (2) **orographic rain & rain shadow** — W Ghats 250–400 cm vs Deccan; Andes: wet S Chile, dry Patagonia; Coast Ranges vs Great Basin · (3) **foehn** — chinook, foehn, zonda · (4) **funnel** — N–S ranges let polar air south (Texas cold waves) and Gulf air north (tornado alley) · (5) **microclimates** — valley breezes, katabatic, inversions, frost, aspect.
- **Aravallis** run SW–NE, parallel to the SW monsoon → little orographic rain → dry W Rajasthan.
- "E–W ranges are walls; N–S ranges are funnels."

## 8. Air masses and fronts (2016)
- **Air mass:** huge body (100s–1000s km), uniform temperature & humidity, from a uniform **source region** under stagnant anticyclone.
- **Bergeron:** c (dry) / m (moist) + A, P, T, E → **cP** Siberia, Canada · **mP** N oceans · **cT** Sahara, Thar (summer) · **mT** tropical seas, Gulf of Mexico.
- **Modification:** cold over warm → unstable, showery · warm over cold → stable, fog, drizzle.
- **Fronts** (Bjerknes, Bergen school ~1918–22): **cold** — steep, cumulonimbus, short heavy rain, colder after · **warm** — gentle, cirrus → stratus, long drizzle, warmer after · **stationary** · **occluded** (cold front overtakes warm). Birthplace of temperate cyclones (G5).
- **Role in macro-climate:** (1) carry heat & moisture between latitudes · (2) seasons = change of dominant air mass (N India: cT/Loo in May → mT/mE monsoon → modified cP + Western Disturbances in winter; Mediterranean: cT summer, mP winter) · (3) fronts breed temperate cyclones → mid-latitude variability · (4) extremes — cP cold waves (polar vortex), persistent cT heat · (5) climate change shifts boundaries.
- Hook: **D-Day 1944** — Stagg's forecast of a gap between fronts.
- "Air masses are the vehicles of climate; fronts are where they collide."

## 9. Climate types, deserts, monsoon climate
- **Köppen** (1900; revised to 1936; vegetation as thermometer): **A** tropical (coldest month >18 °C) · **B** dry · **C** warm temperate · **D** cold snow-forest · **E** polar (warmest <10 °C) · **H** highland. f = no dry season, m = monsoon, w = dry winter, s = dry summer.

| Type | Identifier | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Equatorial (Af) | daily afternoon convection rain, no seasons, tiny range | Amazon, Congo, Indonesia |
| Tropical monsoon (Am) | seasonal wind reversal, wet summer | India, SE Asia |
| Savanna (Aw) | distinct wet and dry season | Sudan, Llanos, Campos |
| Hot desert (BWh) | subtropical high, western margins | Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Sonoran, Great Australian |
| Steppe (BS) | extreme, scanty rain, nomadic herders | Central Asia, Prairies' dry edge |
| Mediterranean (Cs) | hot dry summer, mild wet winter; evergreen oak, olives | Med basin, California, central Chile, Cape, SW Australia |
| China type / humid subtropical (Cfa) | east margin 25–35°, rain all year, summer max | S China, SE USA, SE Brazil |
| West European / Marine West Coast (Cfb) | rain all year 50–250 cm, low range, westerlies | NW Europe, British Columbia, NZ |
| Cool continental / St Lawrence (Dfb), Taiga (Dfc) | harsh winters, east margin/interior | NE USA–Canada, Siberia |
| Tundra (ET) / Ice cap (EF) | warmest month <10 °C / <0 °C | N Canada / Antarctica |
| Highland (H) | vertical zonation | Andes, Himalaya |

- **Deserts at 20–30°, western margins (2013):** (1) subtropical high — subsidence, adiabatic warming, inversion · (2) offshore NE trades on west coasts (onshore and wet on east coasts) · (3) cold currents — **Canary** (Sahara), **Benguela** (Namib/Kalahari), **Humboldt/Peru** (Atacama), **California** (Sonoran), **West Australian** — fog, no rain · (4) continentality, rain shadow. **Atacama = driest non-polar desert.** Eastern margins at same latitude humid (S China, Florida).
- **Monsoon climate feeds Asia (2017):** seasonal wind reversal → (1) rain in the warm season · (2) heat + long days → 2–3 crops · (3) paddy (highest-yield staple) + flood-renewed alluvium (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Mekong, Yangtze, Irrawaddy) · (4) dry sunny winter → rabi crop, harvest, storage · (5) monsoon- and snow-fed rivers → irrigation, labour-intensive farming. Limit: variability — floods, droughts.

## 12. Confusions to avoid
- Horse latitudes = **high** pressure, not low.
- Coriolis is **not** stronger in the SH; SH westerlies are stronger because of less land.
- Westerlies blow 30/35–60° in **each** hemisphere, not 30° N–60° S.
- Harmattan = **West** Africa (not East).
- Foehn/chinook = warming by **descent**; Loo = **advection** of desert heat.
- Mediterranean rain is in **winter**.
- Desert belt = subtropical high + cold currents, **not** warm currents.
- NH cyclones anticlockwise; NH anticyclones clockwise.
- West European (Cfb) = rain all year with low range (2024 answer), not China type.
