# G3 — Atmosphere, Insolation and Temperature
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G3 · everything here is to be memorised

> **The spine.** The Sun heats the **ground**; the ground heats the **air** from below (terrestrial radiation, convection, latent heat); greenhouse gases and clouds decide how much heat escapes. Hence: temperature falls with height (lapse rate) → the troposphere carries the weather → when the lapse rate reverses, **inversions** trap fog and smog → cities add their own heat (**UHI**) → losing ice (the planet's mirror) speeds warming (**cryosphere feedback**).
>
> **Answer spine:** Concept → Energy pathway (draw) → Distribution (latitude · land–sea · height) → Effects → India → Management → Judgement.

## 2. Composition
- **N₂ 78% · O₂ 21% · Ar 0.93% · CO₂ ~0.04% (over 420 ppm; Keeling 1958: ~315 ppm).**
- **Water vapour** 0–4% by volume; maximum in warm tropics; decreases with height and latitude; largest natural GHG; carries **latent heat**.
- **Ozone** 15–35 km — absorbs UV, warms stratosphere. Montreal Protocol 1987; Kigali Amendment 2016 (HFCs); layer recovering.
- **Dust/aerosols** most in dry subtropical and temperate belts; **hygroscopic nuclei** for condensation; scatter light (red sunsets).
- "Minor gases, major heat effects."

## 3. Structure
| Layer | Height | Temperature | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Troposphere | 0 to **~8 km (poles) / ~18 km (equator)** | falls **~6.5 °C/km** | all weather; ~¾ of mass; nearly all vapour; thicker at equator due to convection |
| Tropopause | — | stops falling (~ −56 °C mid-latitudes) | lid on weather; jet streams below |
| Stratosphere | to ~50 km | rises (ozone) | dry, cloud-free, stable — jets cruise in lower part |
| Mesosphere | to ~80–85 km | falls to **~ −90 °C** (coldest) | meteors burn |
| Thermosphere | to 400+ km | rises to 1,000+ °C | **ionosphere 80–400 km reflects radio waves**; aurorae; ISS |
| Exosphere | beyond | — | merges with space |

- **Troposphere determines weather (2022):** (1) holds vapour, nuclei, ~¾ mass · (2) heated from below → convection · (3) rising air cools → dew point → condensation + latent heat → clouds, storms · (4) unequal heating → pressure → winds, air masses, fronts · (5) tropopause caps it; jet streams steer systems. Inversion = weather turns still, foggy, polluted.

## 4. Insolation
- **Solar constant ~1,361 W/m²** (top of atmosphere).
- **Five factors:** Sun's angle (main) · day length · atmospheric transparency · distance (perihelion ~3 Jan, 147 mn km; aphelion ~4 July, 152 mn km — only ~7%) · surface (albedo).
- **Seasons from 23.5° tilt**, axis fixed in direction. 21 June: Sun over Tropic of Cancer; 24 h day north of Arctic Circle. 22 Dec: reverse. Equinoxes ~21 March, ~23 Sept. **Equator always ~12 h day.**
- **Maximum insolation in subtropics** in summer (high Sun + long days + clear skies) → hottest places are subtropical deserts.
- Eratosthenes (~240 BCE) — Sun angle → Earth's circumference.

## 5. Heat budget and heat transfer
- **100 units in: 35 reflected (albedo — 27 clouds, 6 scattering, 2 ice/snow) · 14 absorbed by atmosphere · 51 by surface.**
- **Surface 51 out: 17 straight to space · 34 to atmosphere (19 latent heat, 9 convection/turbulence, 6 long-wave absorbed).**
- **Atmosphere 14 + 34 = 48 → space.** Total out 35 + 17 + 48 = 100.
- **"The atmosphere is heated from below."** (2024 Prelims)
- Greenhouse effect: Earth ~15 °C instead of ~−18 °C (Venus ~465 °C).
- **Latitudinal imbalance:** surplus ~40° N–40° S, deficit poleward → heat carried poleward by winds and ocean currents.
- **Transfer:** radiation · conduction (contact) · convection (vertical; clouds) · advection (horizontal; the Loo).
- **Clouds:** cloudy nights warmer, no dew (clouds return Earth's radiation). Low thick clouds mainly cool (reflect sunlight); high thin cirrus mainly warm (trap IR).
- Earth's albedo ~30–35%; Mercury's lower than Earth's.

## 6. Temperature — controls and distribution
- **Controls:** latitude · altitude (6.5 °C/km) · distance from sea (water: high specific heat, transparent, mixes → small range) · ocean currents (North Atlantic Drift warms Norway; Canary, Benguela cool Africa's west coasts) · winds/air masses · relief and aspect (Himalaya block Siberian cold).
- **Isotherms:** January — bend **equatorward over continents, poleward over oceans** (N); July — reverse; Southern Hemisphere nearly parallel to latitudes.
- **Thermal equator** north of geographic equator on average; shifts with seasons, more over land.
- **Annual range:** smallest over equatorial oceans; largest in continental interiors (**Verkhoyansk: ~−68 °C to 38 °C**) and in NH. **Land–sea contrast sharper in winter (2023 key).**

## 7. Temperature inversion (2013)
- **Definition:** temperature **increases** with height, reversing the normal lapse rate → warm lid → stable air.
- **Types:** radiation (long clear calm winter nights — north India Nov–Feb) · valley (cold air drains downslope — katabatic) · advection (warm air over cold surface — California/Chile fog) · subsidence (sinking air in highs) · frontal (warm over cold at a front).
- **Weather:** no convection, little rain · fog, mist · frost in valleys · smog · low visibility.
- **Inhabitants:** respiratory illness (Delhi winter smog: inversion + calm winds + emissions incl. stubble) · flight/rail delays in Indo-Gangetic plain · crop frost damage · settlements, orchards, tea on warmer mid-slopes (**thermal belt**).
- **London Great Smog, Dec 1952:** ~10,000+ deaths (later estimates) → Clean Air Act 1956.
- "Inversions are natural; smog is man-made."

## 8. Urban heat islands (2013) and heat waves
- **UHI:** city several °C warmer than surroundings, most at night.
- **Causes:** (1) low-albedo, high-heat-capacity surfaces (concrete, asphalt) · (2) lost trees, lakes, wet soil → less evaporative cooling and shade · (3) urban canyons trap radiation, block wind · (4) waste heat — vehicles, ACs, industry · (5) pollution dome traps long-wave. Lost lakes: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai.
- **Effects:** heat stress and deaths (outdoor workers, elderly, slum tin roofs) · power demand · ozone smog · local storms/flash floods · water stress.
- **IMD heat wave:** max ≥40 °C plains (≥37 °C coast, ≥30 °C hills) AND departure 4.5–6.4 °C (severe >6.4 °C); or actual ≥45 °C (severe ≥47 °C).
- **2024** = first calendar year >1.5 °C above pre-industrial (global mean).
- **Gains:** Ahmedabad **Heat Action Plan 2013** (first in South Asia, after May 2010 heat wave ~46.8 °C; deaths fell later) · NDMA heat-wave guidelines · IMD colour-coded warnings · **Telangana Cool Roof Policy 2023** · Ahmedabad slum cool-roof pilots.
- **Gaps:** heat rarely notified as disaster for relief funds · deaths under-counted · plans without money · building codes ignore heat · lakes/green lost · no rights to heat breaks.
- **Reforms (mechanism · institution · accountability):** funded HAPs tied to mitigation funds · NDMA, SDMAs, Urban DMAs · funds against delivery, heat deaths audited · | cool-roof/shade rules in bye-laws and ECBC · MoHUA, BEE, ULBs · occupancy certificates withheld · | notify lakes/wetlands/tree cover · state depts, ULBs · NGT; annual green report · | worker heat breaks · labour depts · penalties, public inspections.

## 9. Moisture
- **Absolute humidity** = vapour per volume · **specific** = per kg of air · **relative** = % of maximum at that temperature. Saturation = 100%; **dew point** = temperature of saturation.
- Condensation on nuclei: **dew** (frost below 0 °C) on surfaces · **fog/mist** near ground · **clouds** aloft.
- Clouds: **cirrus** (high, ice, haloes) · stratus (sheets) · cumulus (heaps) · **cumulonimbus** (towering, rain, thunder). Clouds float — low density.
- **Rain types:** convectional (equator, afternoons; Indian pre-monsoon) · orographic (Western Ghats windward; Deccan & Ladakh rain shadow; **Mawsynram/Cherrapunji** — Khasi Hills) · frontal/cyclonic (Western Disturbances).

## 10. Cryosphere and climate (2017, 2021)
- **Cryosphere** = sea ice, ice sheets, glaciers, snow, lake/river ice, permafrost.
- **Five mechanisms:** (1) **ice–albedo feedback** · (2) sea level — **Antarctic ~58 m, Greenland ~7 m** sea-level equivalent; floating sea ice adds ~nothing · (3) ocean circulation — sea-ice brine makes dense water; meltwater freshens, can weaken **AMOC** · (4) permafrost releases CO₂ and CH₄ · (5) pole–equator gradient → jet stream.
- **Arctic amplification:** Arctic warming **~4× global since 1979** (Rantanen et al. 2022) → weaker gradient → slower, wavier jet → stalled heat waves, cold spells, floods (link debated — write "linked to").
- **Arctic vs Antarctic (2021):** floating sea ice on ocean vs continent under ice sheets. Weather: jet stream & NH extremes vs Southern Ocean & westerlies. Human: Northern Sea Route, North-West Passage, oil/gas/minerals, rivalry, permafrost towns, Indigenous livelihoods vs sea-level rise (coastal cities, deltas, islands; India's 7,500 km coast, Sundarbans, Lakshadweep), krill.
- **NSR hook:** Aug 2017, LNG tanker *Christophe de Margerie* — first unescorted crossing (~19 days).
- **India:** Himadri, Svalbard (2008) · Arctic Policy (2022) · Maitri (1989) · Bharati (2012) · Indian Antarctic Act (2022).
- "The Arctic changes the weather; the Antarctic changes the coastline."

## 13. Confusions to avoid
- Atmosphere heated by **terrestrial**, not solar, radiation.
- **Troposphere** (not "atmosphere") is thicker at the equator — 2004 keyed the "atmosphere" version false.
- Ionosphere, not ozone, reflects radio waves.
- Water vapour is maximum in the tropics, not at the poles.
- High clouds warm; low clouds cool (2022 had them reversed).
- Perihelion is in January — seasons are from tilt.
- Land–sea contrast is sharper in **winter** (2023 key).
- Floating sea-ice melt ≠ sea-level rise; land-ice melt = sea-level rise.
- Inversion = temperature rising with height (not "cold air rising").
