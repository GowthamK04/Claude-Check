# G9 — Indian Monsoon, Soils and Natural Vegetation
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G9 · everything here is to be memorised

> **Three generative ideas.** (1) **Monsoon = giant sea breeze bent by Earth's spin:** land heats faster → heat low over NW India + ITCZ north → SE trades cross equator → Coriolis turns them SW → rain where air rises; winter reverses. (2) **Rising air rains; sinking or already-rained air is dry:** Ghats, NE wet; Deccan interior, Rajasthan dry; TN wet in winter; Purvaiya. (3) **Rain × rock × time = soil; rain (plains) and height (hills) = forest.**
>
> **Spine:** summer sun *so* heat low (land heats faster) → low + northward ITCZ *so* southern trades cross the equator → crossing *so* they turn SW (Coriolis right) → moist air meets mountains/trough *so* it rises, rains, releases latent heat → winter *so* winds reverse, mostly dry (TN coast wet) → rain × rock × time *so* soil type → rainfall on plains, altitude in hills *so* forest type.

## 2. Monsoon mechanism *(generative)*
- **Monsoon** (Arabic *mausim*, season) = seasonal wind reversal, wet summer, dry winter.
- **Classical:** differential heating (Halley 1686) — true but incomplete.
- **Modern:** ITCZ → **monsoon trough** ~20–25°N over Gangetic plain in July; SE trades cross equator → **SW** (Coriolis).
- **Tibet** heating → upper high → **Tropical Easterly Jet** (~15°N, ~14 km).
- **STJ** south of Himalaya in winter → jumps north in early June → "burst".
- **Mascarene High** (S Indian Ocean) · **Somali jet** feeds Arabian Sea branch · latent heat strengthens system (G5 §2).
- **Branches:** Arabian Sea → Western Ghats (orographic), rain-shadow Deccan, Mumbai, Gujarat · Bay of Bengal → Myanmar, NE (Mawsynram, Cherrapunji, Khasi funnel).
- Analogy: giant sea breeze in a spinning room.

## 3. Rainfall and Purvaiya *(generative; GS-I 2023)*
- Bay branch blocked by Himalaya, monsoon trough along plains → turns **west** up the Ganga → arrives in Bhojpur (W Bihar–E UP) from the **east** = **Purvaiya**. Culture: kajari, paddy songs, Chhath, Teej, Shravani; Bhojpuri literature motif.
- Rain **falls E→W** across plains: Kolkata > Patna > Delhi > Rajasthan (2005: Kochi–Kolkata–Patna–Delhi). Duration **falls S→N**.
- **Rain shadow:** Bangalore << Mangalore; Deccan interior.
- Wettest: Mawsynram, Cherrapunji, Ghats crest. Driest: W Rajasthan, Ladakh.
- **TN:** most rain from **NE monsoon (Oct–Dec)** after winds cross the Bay; Chennai 26 °C / 63 cm / 9 °C range.
- **A&N:** humid tropical coastal; both monsoons (2026).

## 4. Seasons and local storms *(leaf)*
- **Winter (Jan–Feb):** anticyclonic north, cold waves, fog; **Western Disturbances** — NW rain/snow, rabi wheat. Anticyclones not caused by winter rain (2001).
- **Hot weather (Mar–May):** heat low NW; **Loo**; dust storms; **Kalbaisakhi/Nor'westers** (Bengal, Assam "tea showers"); **mango showers** (Kerala, Karnataka); **blossom showers** (coffee).
- **SW monsoon (Jun–Sep):** onset Kerala **~1 Jun**; all India **~8 Jul**; withdrawal begins W Rajasthan **~17 Sep**; ends **~15 Oct** (IMD 2020 normals). **Breaks** when trough shifts to foothills (MJO).
- **Post-monsoon (Oct–Dec):** retreating monsoon, **October heat**, Bay cyclones, TN rain.
- Köppen in India: Amw (Konkan), As (TN coast), Aw (Peninsula), BShw, BWhw (Thar), Cwg (Ganga plain), Dfc (Sikkim, Arunachal), E (Ladakh).
- **18 °C January isotherm** divides tropical/subtropical India.

## 5. Monsoon variability and change *(GS-I 2015)*
- **Natural:** ENSO (El Niño weakens often; **2026 = 87% LPA, NE lowest since 1901**) · IOD (+ helps) · MJO (active/break) · Eurasian snow (heavy → weak) · Atlantic patterns.
- **Human ("humanising landscapes"):** aerosols/brown cloud dim land → weaker contrast · deforestation cuts moisture recycling; NW irrigation cools land · urban heat islands → extreme local storms (Mumbai 2005, Chennai 2015) · GHG warming ~7%/°C → fewer moderate, more extreme days; **widespread extremes over central India ~3× 1950–2015 (Roxy et al. 2017)**.
- Verdict: agree for trends and extremes; year-to-year still oceanic.
- Monsoon and life: kharif/rabi, ~half net sown area rain-fed, recharge, rural demand & inflation, floods/droughts, cultural unity.

## 6. Soils *(generative)*
- **Factors:** parent rock, climate, relief, organisms, time. **Leaching** = rain carrying soluble minerals down.
- **Rain-forest soils poor** (leaching, fast decomposition). **Red = ferric oxides.** Organic matter **raises** water holding; soil in sulphur cycle; irrigation → salinisation.
| Soil | Formation | Where | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alluvial (~40%) | river deposits; potash-rich, N & P poor; khadar/bhangar | Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, deltas | rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute |
| Black (regur) | weathered Deccan basalt (fissure lava); clay; lime, Fe, Mg; moisture-retentive; self-ploughing | MH, MP, Gujarat, Telangana | cotton, soybean, jowar |
| Red & yellow | crystalline granite/gneiss; iron oxide; poor N, P, humus | E & S Deccan, Odisha, Chhattisgarh | millets, pulses, groundnut |
| Laterite | intense leaching; Fe, Al left; acidic; poor N, K; hardens (brick) | W Ghats, Kerala, KA, Odisha, Meghalaya | cashew, tapioca, tea, coffee |
| Arid | sandy, saline, low humus, kankar | W Rajasthan | bajra, pulses (irrigated) |
| Saline/alkaline (usar, reh, kallar) | salts drawn up; over-irrigation; impermeable | Punjab–Haryana canals, Kachchh, deltas | gypsum, drainage |
| Peaty/marshy | waterlogged, organic, acidic | Kuttanad (Kerala), Sundarbans | rice |
| Forest/mountain | thin, altitude-dependent | Himalaya, W Ghats | tea, coffee, spices, fruit |

## 7. Erosion and conservation
- **Splash → sheet → rill → gully** (Chambal ravines).
- Causes: **deforestation**, overgrazing, jhum, faulty irrigation (waterlogging, salinity), construction, mining, wind.
- Fixes: **contour bunding/ploughing, terracing, strip cropping** (slopes) · gully plugging, afforestation (ravines) · **shelter belts, wind breaks, sand fences**, stubble mulch (deserts) · crop rotation, cover crops.
- ISRO 2021: ~30% land degraded (G2) · Soil Health Card 2015 · PMKSY watersheds · NMSA · 26 Mha by 2030.

## 8. Natural vegetation *(generative; GS-I 2023)*
| Rain | Type | Where | Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| >200 cm | Tropical evergreen & semi-evergreen | W Ghats, NE (Arunachal, Mizoram), A&N | rosewood, mahogany, ebony |
| 100–200 | Moist deciduous | NE foothills, E Peninsula, Ghats lee | teak, sal, bamboo |
| 50–100 | Dry deciduous (**most widespread**) | plateau interiors | teak, tendu, peepal |
| <50 | Thorn & scrub | Rajasthan, Gujarat, W Andhra | babul, kikar, cactus |
| coasts | Mangroves | Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika, Pichavaram, Godavari–Krishna, Achra (MH), Coondapur (KA), Vembanad (Kerala) | sundari |
- **Montane:** tropical/moist deciduous (<~1,000 m) → subtropical chir pine (~1,000–2,000) → temperate oak, deodar, fir, spruce → alpine birch, juniper, rhododendron → snow; **each zone higher in the east** (warmer, wetter). Mahogany not Himalayan.
- Champion & Seth 1968: 16 type groups.
- **Diversity factors (2023):** rainfall (10–1,100 cm) · temperature & latitude · relief & altitude · soil · crossroads of Indo-Malayan, Ethiopian, Palaearctic realms + isolated islands · human use.
- **Sanctuaries in rain forests:** hotspots (W Ghats, Indo-Burma, Sundaland–Nicobar) · endemics (lion-tailed macaque — Silent Valley; hoolock gibbon — Hollongapar; Nilgiri tahr; Malabar civet) · watersheds · carbon on poor soils · tribal livelihoods, ecotourism · wild crop relatives · threats: fragmentation, plantations, roads, dams, small size, conflict, ESZ disputes.

## 9. Forests and climate change *(GS-I 2020)*
- **ISFR 2023 (FSI, Dec 2024):** forest + tree **8,27,357 km² = 25.17%** (forest **21.76%**, tree **3.41%**) vs **33%** goal (NFP 1988) · mangroves **4,992 km²** (WB largest) · carbon **~7,285 Mt** · sink **30.43 bn t CO₂e = 2.29 bn t above 2005** vs NDC **2.5–3 bn t by 2030** · 19 states/UTs >33%; 8 >75% · highest share Lakshadweep, Mizoram, A&N · largest area MP, Arunachal, Chhattisgarh · WG ESA & NE lost cover · fires worst Uttarakhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh · sal most growing stock.
- Caveat: "forest cover" = ≥1 ha, ≥10% canopy, includes plantations/orchards.
- **Climate links:** sink · source (loss, fire) · monsoon moisture recycling, cooling, watersheds · adaptation (mangroves vs cyclones, slopes vs landslides) · vulnerability (upslope shifts, fires, pests).
- **Policy:** Green India Mission · CAMPA · Nagar Van Yojana · **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 2023** (criticised; SC 2024–25: keep dictionary meaning of "forest") · FRA 2006.
