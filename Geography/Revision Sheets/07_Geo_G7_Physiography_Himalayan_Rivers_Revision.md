# G7 — India's Physiography and Himalayan Rivers
### Revision sheet · Geography · Phase 2 · Unit G7 · everything here is to be memorised

> **Three generative ideas.** (1) **Three blocks of different age:** old stable Peninsula (Gondwana) · young rising Himalaya · new flat Plains. (2) **Young mountains are unstable, old plateaus worn:** gorges, quakes, landslides vs low plateaus, rifts, lava. (3) **Himalayan rivers are older than the range and ice-fed:** antecedent gorges, perennial flow, heavy silt — now threatened by glacier loss.
>
> **Spine:** India broke from Gondwana *so* drifted north and hit Asia → collision *so* Tethys sediments squeezed into parallel ranges on thrust faults → mountain load bends the plate *so* a trough forms and rivers fill it (Plains) → old Peninsula worn, cracked, flooded by lava *so* plateaus, rifts, Deccan Trap → Peninsula tilts east *so* big rivers flow east → Himalayan rivers older than the range, glacier-fed *so* gorges, perennial flow, silt → young steep slopes + heavy rain + cutting *so* landslides, GLOFs, subsidence.

## 2. Frame and location
- **Six divisions:** Himalaya · Northern Plains · Peninsular Plateau · Thar · Coastal Plains · Islands.
- **Extent:** 8°4′N–37°6′N, 68°7′E–97°25′E · 3.28 mn km² (~2.4% of world land, 7th) · **Tropic of Cancer through 8 states** (Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, WB, Tripura, Mizoram) · **IST 82°30′E** (Mirzapur), GMT+5:30.
- **Coastline 11,098.81 km** (Survey of India/NHO, 2024–25; old 7,516.6 km) — methodology, not new land; **Gujarat longest (~2,340 km)**; TN now > AP; review every 10 years. **9 coastal states.**
- **Subcontinent (2021):** physical separateness (Himalaya, Hindu Kush, Purvanchal, seas) · own plate (Gondwana fragment) · size & every landform/climate · monsoon unity · cultural diversity.
- Analogy: smashed layer cake.

## 3. Himalaya
| Range | Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Trans-Himalaya | ~6,000 m | Karakoram (K2 8,611 m), Ladakh, Zaskar |
| Great (Himadri) | avg ~6,000 m | granite core, glaciers; Kanchenjunga 8,586 m |
| Lesser (Himachal) | 3,700–4,500 m | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat; hill stations; Kashmir, Kangra valleys |
| Shiwalik | 900–1,100 m | loose gravel; duns (Dehradun) |
- **Thrusts:** MCT, MBT, MFT (G2 quakes, seismic gap).
- **W→E (Burrard):** Punjab/Kashmir (Indus–Sutlej) · Kumaon (Sutlej–Kali) · Nepal (Kali–Teesta) · Assam (Teesta–Dihang). **Purvanchal:** Patkai, Naga, Manipur, Mizo → **Arakan Yoma (Myanmar)**.
- Wider in west, steeper rise in east. Nanda Devi = Kumaon; Namcha Barwa = Assam Himalaya; Nokrek = Garo (Meghalaya).
- **Roles:** block Siberian cold · hold monsoon · perennial rivers · alluvium · forests, hydro, tourism · defence · hazards. Himalaya > 5 states; W Ghats 6.

## 4. Northern Plains
- Trough in front of rising Himalaya filled with alluvium (hundreds–thousands of m); ~3,200 km long, 150–300 km wide.
- **Bhabar** (pebbles, 8–16 km, streams sink) → **Terai** (marshy, streams re-emerge) → **Bhangar** (older alluvium, terraces, kankar, less fertile) / **Khadar** (new floodplain, most fertile) → **delta** (Sundarbans).
- Punjab plain (doabs) · Ganga plain · Brahmaputra plain. Green Revolution, dense population; floods, waterlogging, groundwater decline.

## 5. Peninsula and Deccan Trap
- **Central Highlands** (N of Narmada): Aravalli (oldest fold, worn; **Guru Shikhar, Mt Abu, Rajasthan**), Malwa, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Vindhya, Chota Nagpur. **Deccan** south of Narmada.
- **Rift valleys:** Narmada, Tapi (flow west between Vindhya & Satpura), Damodar (coal).
- **W Ghats (Sahyadri):** continuous, steep, **higher in south** — Anaimudi 2,695 m (highest in Peninsula); Doddabetta (Nilgiri); Palghat gap. **E Ghats:** broken, lower; Nallamala, Velikonda, Javadi, **Shevaroy (TN)**. **Nilgiri:** Ghats meet.
- **Meghalaya plateau** (Garo, Khasi, Jaintia), Karbi Anglong — Peninsular block; Malda gap.
- **Deccan Trap (2022):** ~66 Ma fissure basalts (Réunion hotspot), ~5 lakh km²; "trap" = steps. Resources: **black cotton soil** (cotton, soybean, sugarcane, jowar) · **poor basalt aquifers** → drought, need watershed works · **bauxite** in laterite caps (Kolhapur, Amarkantak) · building stone, zeolites · **Koyna** hydro · **Ajanta–Ellora, Lonar crater, Mahabaleshwar**. Limits: thin slope soils, erosion, cracking clays, reservoir seismicity (Koyna 1967). "Rich in soil, poor in water."

## 6. Thar
- West of Aravalli (parallel to monsoon → little rain); dunes (barchans); **Luni → Rann of Kachchh**; inland drainage; **salt lakes Sambhar (near Jaipur), Didwana, Kuchaman, Sargol, Khatu**. Rann = repeated sea-level fall. Ladakh = cold desert.

## 8. Himalayan rivers
- **Antecedent:** Indus, **Sutlej**, Dihang, Arun, Kali — older than uplift; gorges.
- **Himalayan vs Peninsular:** glacier+monsoon vs rain · perennial vs seasonal · gorges vs broad valleys · meander/shift (Kosi) vs fixed · heavy silt vs light · antecedent/dendritic vs radial/trellis.
- **Indus:** rises near Mansarovar; Jhelum (Wular) · Chenab (largest; Chandra+Bhaga) · Ravi · Beas (rises India, Rohtang) · Sutlej (Tibet, Rakas Tal). Jhelum+Ravi→Chenab; Beas→Sutlej; Chenab+Sutlej = Panjnad → Indus; **Sutlej joins directly**. Others: Shyok, Zaskar, Gilgit, Spiti. N→S Shyok–Zaskar–Spiti–Sutlej. Indus Waters Treaty 1960.
- **Ganga:** Bhagirathi (Gaumukh/Gangotri) + Alaknanda at **Devprayag**; prayags Vishnu-, Nand-, Karna-, Rudraprayag; Yamuna (Yamunotri/Bandarpunch; Chambal, Sind, Betwa, Ken) at **Prayagraj**; north bank W→E **Gomti–Ghaghara–Gandak–Kosi**; south: Son (Amarkantak), Damodar; Padma (Bangladesh), Hooghly (Farakka); Sundarbans largest delta.
- **Brahmaputra:** Tsangpo (Chemayungdung glacier) east across Tibet → **U-turn (syntaxis) at Namcha Barwa** → **Dihang** + Dibang + Lohit near Sadiya; north bank Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Sankosh, **Teesta** (Rangeet from Sikkim); south bank Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri; braided; **Majuli**; Jamuna in Bangladesh. Barak not in Arunachal.
- **Lakes:** Wular (Jhelum, largest freshwater) · Kanwar Tal (Burhi Gandak) · Kolleru (between deltas, stream-fed).
- **Hydro:** Tehri (Bhagirathi) · Tapovan–Vishnugad (Dhauliganga) · Dul Hasti, Baglihar (Chenab).

## 9. Glaciers
- **Pairs:** Siachen–Nubra (N of Nubra) · Gangotri–Bhagirathi · Bandarpunch–Yamuna · Bara Shigri–Chenab · Zemu–Teesta · Milam–Gori Ganga.
- **ICIMOD 2023:** HKH glaciers lost ice ~65% faster in 2010s vs 2000s; 30–50% volume loss by 2100 even at 1.5–2 °C.
- **2014 symptoms:** snout retreat (Gangotri), rising snowline, less snow, altered monsoon/WDs, more cloudbursts/extremes, tree line & apples moving up.
- **2020 water impacts:** short-term more melt → floods, GLOFs, silt · long-term after **peak water** → lower dry-season flow; **Indus most exposed**, Brahmaputra least · irrigation, hydropower, drinking water, groundwater recharge · IWT, Chinese dams on Tsangpo.
- **GLOF:** **South Lhonak, Sikkim, Oct 2023** → Teesta-III dam destroyed. **Chamoli 7 Feb 2021** rock-ice avalanche (Rishiganga; 200+ dead/missing).
- Responses: NMSHE (NAPCC) · NDMA GLOF guidelines 2020 + national GLOF mitigation · EWS · monitoring (NCPOR, Wadia).

## 10. Landslides and fragility
- **Himalaya:** young, rising; quakes on thrusts; fractured rock, loose Shiwalik; moraines; river undercutting; cloudbursts, WD+monsoon; roads, tunnels, dams, deforestation → frequent, year-round, quake+rain.
- **W Ghats:** old, stable, but 250–400 cm monsoon in bursts; thick laterite on hard rock = slip plane; steep scarp; plantations, quarries, slope homes, soil piping → seasonal rain-triggered debris flows. Gadgil 2011, Kasturirangan 2013.
- ~12–15% of land landslide-prone · **ISRO Landslide Atlas 2023: Rudraprayag, Tehri Garhwal top** · Joshimath Jan 2023 (subsidence) · Silkyara Nov 2023 (41 rescued after 17 days) · Himachal 2023, 2025 · **Wayanad 30 Jul 2024 (200+ dead)** · Malin 2014 · Kedarnath 2013.
- **Zonation (GS-III 2019):** grade risk by slope, geology, soil, rain, land use → planning, building, slope works, sensors, insurance; GSI national susceptibility mapping.
- **NLRMS (NDMA 2019; GS-III 2021):** hazard mapping · monitoring & EWS · awareness · capacity building · techno-legal regime · mountain-zone focus · mitigation.
- **GS-III 2026:** development need vs slope cutting, muck dumping, overloading (Char Dham, Joshimath, cascades) → carrying capacity, strategic/cumulative EIA, tunnelling rules, EWS, local participation.
- **Reforms (mechanism · institution · accountability):** binding zonation in permits · state planning depts, GSI, SDMAs · void permits, disaster audits | cumulative valley EIA · MoEFCC, CWC, NHAI/BRO · conditional clearances, NGT | sensor EWS · GSI, NDMA, IMD · published performance | town carrying capacity · states, ULBs · annual report to legislature.
