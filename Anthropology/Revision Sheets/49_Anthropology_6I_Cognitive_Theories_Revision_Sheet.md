## 1. What it is

**Plain version:** describe a culture the way its own members carve up the world, and write that down precisely enough for a stranger to use it.

- **Definition:** the study of the relation between culture and thought. It treats culture as a **shared system of knowledge**, described in the **categories and organising principles of its bearers**, usually through their language.
- **Goodenough (1957) — word for word:** ==a society's culture consists of whatever it is one has to know or believe in order to operate in a manner acceptable to its members.==
- **Tyler (1969, p. 3) — two questions:** ==what material phenomena are significant for the people of a culture, and how do they organise them?==
- **Tyler's formula:** cultures are **not material phenomena** but ==**cognitive organisations of material phenomena**.==
- **Other names:**
  - **ethnoscience** — Sturtevant 1964: "the system of knowledge and cognition typical of a given culture";
  - **new ethnography** — Harris's *critical* label;
  - **ethnosemantics**, **componential analysis**, **folk classification**.
- **Position:** the **ideational** wing of American anthropology, against the materialists (White, Steward, Harris).

## 2. Roots

- **Boas** — native categories and language as the key to culture. Malinowski's "native's point of view" (*Argonauts*, 1922) is the same aim.
- **Sapir–Whorf** — the school took the weak version: vocabulary shows what a culture distinguishes.
- **Structural linguistics** — phonemics keeps only the sound contrasts that matter to speakers.
- **Kenneth Pike (1954)** — *Language in Relation to a Unified Theory of the Structure of Human Behavior*. He coined **emic/etic** from phonemic/phonetic.
  - **emic** = distinctions meaningful within the system;
  - **etic** = the outsider's cross-cultural grid.
- **Goodenough 1956** (Trukese kin terms) and **Lounsbury 1956** (Pawnee kin terms) — the first componential analyses.
- **The Yale cohort** — Conklin, Frake and Sturtevant were graduate students together in the early 1950s.

## 3. Core propositions

1. Culture is a **code in the mind**; behaviour and artefacts are its products.
2. Each culture organises the world **uniquely** (Tyler).
3. Categories are built **by contrast**, on the phonemic principle.
4. **Language is the royal road** — lexemes label categories.
5. Description must be **replicable** — formal procedures (Sturtevant).
6. **Ethnography as a grammar of culture** — it should let an outsider behave appropriately. Frake, "How to Ask for a Drink in Subanun" (1964).
7. **Emics before comparison** — compare only units that are truly comparable (Tyler).

## 4. Method — the tool-kit

- **Elicitation frames** — "What kind of X is that?" Frake, "The Ethnographic Study of Cognitive Systems" (1962).
- **Domain** — things under one cover term. **Contrast set** — terms on one level that exclude one another.
- **Folk taxonomy** — vertical, **"kind of"**: plant › tree › oak › white oak.
  - Berlin's ranks: unique beginner › life form › **generic** › specific › varietal.
  - The generic rank is the most salient and the first learnt (Berlin, Breedlove & Raven 1973).
- **Paradigm / componential analysis** — horizontal: each term is a bundle of **distinctive features**.
- **Wallace & Atkins 1960**, "The Meaning of Kinship Terms" — English kin terms on three dimensions:

| term | sex | generation | lineality |
|---|---|---|---|
| father | male | +1 | lineal |
| uncle | male | +1 | colineal |
| brother | male | 0 | colineal |
| aunt | female | +1 | colineal |
| cousin | **unmarked** | **any** | ablineal |

- **Keys / decision tables** — Frake 1961, "The Diagnosis of Disease among the Subanun of Mindanao".
- **Survivors** — free-lists, pile sorts, triads, consensus analysis. Weller & Romney, *Systematic Data Collection* (1988).

## 5. Conklin (1926–2016) — the exemplar

- **Fieldwork:** Berkeley, then Yale (PhD 1954); taught at Columbia, then Yale.
  - **Hanunóo** (Mangyan) of **Mindoro, Philippines**, 1947–58;
  - **Ifugao** of Luzon, 1961–73.
- ***The Relation of Hanunóo Culture to the Plant World* (PhD 1954)**
  - ==**about 1,600 plant types** (1,625), more than the botanists' species for the same flora by over 400.==
  - This turned ethnobotany from lists of "useful plants" into the study of folk classification.
- **"Hanunóo Color Categories" (1955)** — four terms on two dimensions: **light vs dark**, and **wet/fresh vs dry/ripe**.
  - **mabiru** — dark, black;
  - **malagti'** — light, white;
  - **marara'** — maroon, red, orange, yellow (dry, ripe matter);
  - **malatuy** — light green and its mixtures with yellow and light brown (fresh growth).
  - The system looks deficient on a spectral chart but is exact once you see it is **built for plants**.
- ***Hanunóo Agriculture* (FAO 1957)** — "an integral system of shifting cultivation": swidden is **rational and knowledge-intensive**. Credited with the term **ethnoecology**.
- **"Lexicographical Treatment of Folk Taxonomies" (1962)** · *Folk Classification* bibliography (1972) · *Ethnographic Atlas of Ifugao* (1980).
- **Why he matters:** proof that the method works and that **indigenous knowledge is systematic**.

## 6. Tyler (1932–2020) — the definer who became the critic

- **Career:** UC Davis, Tulane, then **Rice University** (first chair, 1970).
- **Fieldwork:** the **Koya of the Godavari valley, Andhra Pradesh**, from the early 1960s.
  - *Koya: An Outline Grammar* (Gommu dialect, 1969);
  - *India: An Anthropological Perspective* (1973).
- **Koya kinship:** "Koya Language Morphology and Patterns of Kinship Behavior" (1965) and "Context and Variation in Koya Kinship Terminology" (1966).
  - Term use **varies with context**, so no single context-free paradigm fits usage. This was the seed of his doubt.
- ***Cognitive Anthropology* (ed., 1969)** — the reader that named the field. Its introduction gives the two questions, the "cognitive organisations" formula, and "emics before comparison".
- **"The Myth of P: Epistemology and Formal Analysis" (1969)** — questioned what formal analyses actually represent.
- ***The Said and the Unsaid: Mind, Meaning and Culture* (1978)** — meaning lies in context, intention and the unsaid, so he rejected formalism.
- **"Post-Modern Ethnography: From Document of the Occult to Occult Document"** in *Writing Culture* (1986) — ethnography should **evoke, not represent**. He attended the 1984 Santa Fe seminar. *The Unspeakable* (1987). **→ 6-J**
- **Geertz, "Thick Description" (1973)** — calls locating culture in heads the ==**"cognitivist fallacy"**== and quotes **Tyler** as its spokesman.

> **Answer line:** "Tyler's career is the arc of the school: he defined it in 1969, found in Koya kinship that usage escapes formal paradigms, and by 1986 argued that ethnography can only evoke."

## 7. Three phases

| Phase | Character | Names |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Ethnoscience, c. 1956–70 | Emic, particular, formal; bounded domains | Goodenough, Lounsbury, Conklin, Frake, Sturtevant, Tyler 1969 |
| 2 · Universals, c. 1969–80 | The method found cross-cultural regularities | **Berlin & Kay 1969**, Berlin 1973, Rosch (prototypes) |
| 3 · Schemas and models, 1980s on | From words to cultural models; consensus measured; cognition in practice | Holland & Quinn 1987; Romney, Weller & Batchelder 1986; D'Andrade 1995; Hutchins 1995 |

- **Berlin & Kay, *Basic Color Terms* (1969)** — eleven basic terms, added in a fixed order:
  - I black/white → II red → III–IV green, yellow → V blue → VI brown → VII purple, pink, orange, grey.
- **Pair it with Conklin:** relativism of category **boundaries**, universality of **focal** colours.

> **Answer line:** "A school founded to prove every culture unique produced anthropology's best evidence for universals."

## 8. Criticism — the ledger (name at least four)

| Criticism | Point | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychological reality** | Several solutions fit the same terms; whose model is it? | Wallace & Atkins 1960; **Burling 1964, "Cognition and Componential Analysis: God's Truth or Hocus-Pocus?"** |
| Formalism, narrow domains | Works for kin, colour, plants and disease; silent on religion, politics, change | Harris 1968; general |
| Lexical bias | Tacit, practical, embodied knowledge is never put into words | Bloch 1991; Tyler 1978 |
| Idealism, no explanation | Emics without etics; describes but does not explain | **Harris, *The Rise of Anthropological Theory* (1968)** |
| Culture is public | Meaning lives in shared symbols, not in heads | **Geertz 1973** |
| Variation and power | Whose categories? Gender, age, expertise hidden | Feminist and practice critics; answered by consensus theory |
| Static | No history or change | General |
| Elicitation artefacts | The question frame shapes the taxonomy | Tyler 1966, 1969 |

**Verdict:** ==a failed grand theory, a surviving method==. Its demonstration that indigenous knowledge is systematic is now policy common sense.

## 9. Comparisons

- **Cognitive vs structuralism**
  - Both take meaning by contrast from linguistics.
  - Cognitive anthropology seeks **conscious, named, particular** categories.
  - Lévi-Strauss seeks **unconscious, universal** binary structures.
- **Cognitive vs interpretive (Geertz)**
  - Cognitive: culture in heads, formal models, the aim is to predict behaviour.
  - Interpretive: culture in public symbols, thick description, the aim is meaning.
- **Emic vs etic** — both are **Pike's** terms. Cognitive anthropology champions emic; Harris champions etic.
- **Conklin vs Tyler**
  - Conklin is the exemplar: Philippines; plants, colour, farming; legacy in ethnobiology and traditional ecological knowledge.
  - Tyler is the definer turned critic: Koya, India; kinship and language; legacy in postmodern ethnography and rhetoric.

## 10. India

- **Tyler among the Koya** (Godavari valley) — the clause's own scholar did his fieldwork in India.
- **Dravidian cross/parallel kin terms** — Dumont 1953 (terminology expresses marriage alliance); Trautmann, *Dravidian Kinship* (1981).
- **Ethnobotany** — S. K. Jain, Botanical Survey of India.
- **Recording traditional knowledge** — People's Biodiversity Registers under the Biological Diversity Act 2002; the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (2001).
- **Jhum** — P. S. Ramakrishnan, *Shifting Agriculture and Sustainable Development* (1992), Meghalaya. Use with the 2022 Paper II question.
- **Folk illness categories** — hot/cold humoral classifications matter in health communication.

## The traps

1. **Tyler** (Stephen, cognitive, 1932–2020) is **not Tylor** (Edward, evolutionist, 6-A).
2. **Emic/etic is Pike's** (1954); Harris borrowed the terms.
3. **"New ethnography"** is Harris's critical label, not the school's banner.
4. **Conklin worked in the Philippines** (Hanunóo, Ifugao), not India. Tyler worked in India.
5. **Cognitive ≠ structuralism** — conscious and particular, not unconscious and universal.
6. **Write "about 1,600"** for the Hanunóo plant figure; sources vary.
