## 1. What it is

**Plain version:** suspicion of big confident stories, and of anyone who claims to tell the whole truth about other people.

- **Definition:** a late-twentieth-century movement that rejects universal truths and grand theories, treats knowledge and identity as ==constructed, plural and bound up with power==, and attends to language, representation and the position of the knower.
- **Lyotard, *The Postmodern Condition: A Report on Knowledge* (1979):** ==the postmodern is "incredulity toward metanarratives"== — progress, reason, science, Marxism.
- **Postmodernity vs postmodernism:**
  - *postmodernity* is the condition: post-industrial, globalised, media-saturated. Jameson, *Postmodernism, or, the Cultural Logic of Late Capitalism* (1991).
  - *postmodernism* is the theory.
  - *post-structuralism* is its French philosophical core.
- **In anthropology:** less a theory of culture than a ==critique of how anthropology knows and writes== — the reflexive, literary turn of the 1980s.

## 3. Why anthropology turned — the pressures

- **Asad (ed.), *Anthropology and the Colonial Encounter* (1973)** — the discipline grew inside colonial power.
- **Malinowski, *A Diary in the Strict Sense of the Term* (1967)** — the "objective observer" was a pose.
- **Said, *Orientalism* (1978).**
- **Fabian, *Time and the Other* (1983)** — the ==**denial of coevalness**== (allochronism): living people placed in a "primitive" past.
- **Geertz's textual turn** — culture as text (1973); *Works and Lives: The Anthropologist as Author* (1988).
- **Experimental ethnographies:**
  - Rabinow, *Reflections on Fieldwork in Morocco* (1977);
  - Crapanzano, *Tuhami: Portrait of a Moroccan* (1980);
  - Shostak, *Nisa* (1981);
  - Dwyer, *Moroccan Dialogues* (1982).
- **Feminism** — whose experience counted as "the culture"?

## 4. The canon

- **Santa Fe seminar 1984** (School of American Research) produced **Clifford & Marcus (eds.), *Writing Culture: The Poetics and Politics of Ethnography* (1986)**.
- **Clifford, "Partial Truths":** ethnographic truths are ==**"inherently partial — committed and incomplete"**==. Ethnography is writing: rhetorical, institutional, generic, political, historical.
- **Clifford, "On Ethnographic Authority" (1983)** — four modes of authority:
  - **experiential** ("I was there");
  - **interpretive**;
  - **dialogic**;
  - **polyphonic**.
- **Marcus & Fischer, *Anthropology as Cultural Critique: An Experimental Moment in the Human Sciences* (1986)** — a ==**"crisis of representation"**==; an experimental moment; anthropology as **cultural critique** of one's own society.
- **Tyler, "Post-Modern Ethnography: From Document of the Occult to Occult Document" (1986)** — ethnography should **evoke, not represent**. He had defined cognitive anthropology in 1969 (← 6-I).
- **Rosaldo, *Culture and Truth* (1989)** — the **positioned subject**. He understood Ilongot headhunters' grief and rage only after his wife's death.
- **Abu-Lughod, "Writing against Culture" (1991)** — "a culture" fixes difference and hierarchy; write ethnographies of the particular.
- **Gupta & Ferguson, "Beyond 'Culture'" (1992); *Anthropological Locations* (1997)** — culture is not tied to a place; the field/home divide is constructed.
- **Marcus, "Ethnography in/of the World System: The Emergence of Multi-Sited Ethnography" (1995)** — follow the people, the thing, the metaphor, the conflict.
- **Appadurai (1990; *Modernity at Large* 1996)** — five scapes of global flow.

## 5. The basic features (the 2015 question) — attach a name to each

| # | Feature | Anthropologist |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crisis of representation | Marcus & Fischer 1986 |
| 2 | No master paradigm (evolutionism, functionalism, structuralism dethroned) | Lyotard; Marcus & Fischer |
| 3 | Ethnography as text; truths partial | Clifford 1986; Geertz 1988 |
| 4 | Reflexivity — the author's position | Rabinow 1977; Rosaldo 1989 |
| 5 | Polyphony and dialogue — informants as co-authors | Clifford 1983; Crapanzano; Dwyer |
| 6 | Culture contested, hybrid, open — not a bounded whole | Abu-Lughod 1991; Gupta & Ferguson 1992 |
| 7 | Power/knowledge; colonial critique | Foucault; Asad; Said; Fabian |
| 8 | Epistemological relativism | Tyler (strong); Clifford (moderate) |
| 9 | Experimental writing — life histories, dialogue, evocation | Shostak; Tyler |
| 10 | Multi-sited, global | Marcus 1995; Appadurai 1996 |

> **Answer line:** "Postmodern anthropology is less a new theory of culture than a new theory of ethnography: it asks who has the authority to describe a culture, and how."

**Realist vs postmodern ethnography (Fig. 1) — eight bases:**

| Basis | Realist (to c. 1970) | Postmodern (1980s on) |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | "I was there"; objective | Questioned; **reflexive** |
| Voice | One | **Polyphony**, dialogue |
| Culture | Bounded whole | Contested, open |
| Time | Ethnographic present | History, coevalness |
| Theory | Master paradigm | No metanarrative |
| Text | Transparent window | Rhetoric — **partial truths** |
| Aim | Science of society | Evocation; critique |
| Exemplar | *Argonauts* 1922; *The Nuer* 1940 | *Writing Culture* 1986 |

## 6. Derrida and deconstruction (the 2024 question)

- **Jacques Derrida (1930–2004).**
  - **"Structure, Sign and Play in the Discourse of the Human Sciences"** — Johns Hopkins, October 1966.
  - ***Of Grammatology***, ***Writing and Difference***, *Speech and Phenomena* — all 1967.
- **Deconstruction is ==a practice of reading, not destruction==.**
- **Concepts:**
  - **logocentrism** — the belief in a fixed centre of meaning (reason, truth, the speaking subject);
  - **phonocentrism** — speech privileged over writing;
  - **binary oppositions** are hierarchies: one term is privileged, the other is defined as its lack;
  - ==***différance*** = to differ + to defer==: meaning comes from difference between signs and is endlessly deferred;
  - **trace** — each sign carries traces of others;
  - **supplement** — what is added as secondary turns out to be necessary;
  - ==**"Il n'y a pas de hors-texte" — "there is nothing outside the text"**== means nothing reaches us outside some system of signs and context. It does **not** mean the world is unreal.
- **The four steps:**
  1. **Identify** the binary (A/B).
  2. **Expose** the hierarchy (A > B).
  3. **Reverse** it — A depends on B (B ⊂ A).
  4. **Displace** it — the opposition is unstable (A ⇄ B).
- **Derrida on Lévi-Strauss — the line that lifts the answer:**
  - **1966:** structure needs a centre that is itself outside the structure's play. Deny the centre and there is only free play. The **engineer/bricoleur** contrast collapses, because all thought is bricolage.
  - **"The Violence of the Letter: From Lévi-Strauss to Rousseau"** (in *Of Grammatology*): Lévi-Strauss's Nambikwara "writing lesson" in *Tristes Tropiques* (1955) linked writing to domination. Derrida replies that this romanticises an innocent oral society, since violence and classification precede alphabetic writing ("arche-writing"). The result is ==**ethnocentrism in reverse**==.
- **Anthropological uses:**
  - ethnography read as text (Clifford's partial truths; Tyler's evocation);
  - binaries to deconstruct — civilised/primitive, modern/traditional, writing/speech, culture/nature, observer/observed, home/field, self/Other, emic/etic;
  - "tribe", "caste" and "primitive" as colonial constructions (Cohn; Dirks).
- **Critique:**
  - obscurity and textualism — material inequality recedes;
  - relativism and nihilism (Habermas);
  - political quietism — though Derrida later wrote "Force of Law" (1990) on justice;
  - "nothing outside the text" is often misread;
  - it suits texts better than fieldwork (Spiro, Harris).

## 7. Postmodernism and the marginalised (the 2025 question)

**GIVES**
- **Deconstructs dominant narratives** — development as a discourse. Escobar, *Encountering Development* (1995); Ferguson, *The Anti-Politics Machine* (1990, Lesotho).
- **Power/knowledge: whose knowledge counts** — indigenous knowledge legitimised (FRA 2006).
- **Categories unmade** — the Criminal Tribes Act 1871, repealed 1952 (denotified tribes); PTG renamed **PVTG**.
- **Voice and polyphony** — Subaltern Studies (Ranajit Guha, from 1982).
- **Situated knowledge** — Haraway 1988; ==**Gopal Guru (2002): "theoretical Brahmins and empirical Shudras"**==.
- **Reflexive, collaborative research** — consent, partnership.
- **Recognition of plural identities** — Butler 1990; Dalit and Adivasi assertion.

**COSTS**
- **Relativism undercuts rights** — human rights are a metanarrative the poor need.
- **Fragmentation** weakens solidarity.
- **Discourse over land, wages and violence** (Harris 1999).
- **Elitist prose** — Nussbaum on Butler, "The Professor of Parody" (1999).
- ==**Spivak, "Can the Subaltern Speak?" (1988)**== — intellectuals who "give voice" may speak over the subaltern.

**RESOLUTION**
- **Strategic essentialism** (Spivak) — use an identity politically while knowing it is constructed.
- **Ethical, militant anthropology** — Scheper-Hughes, "The Primacy of the Ethical" (1995).
- **Voice made law** — PESA 1996, FRA 2006.

> **Indian case — Niyamgiri (2013):** the Supreme Court (*Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF*) let the **Dongria Kondh gram sabhas** decide whether bauxite mining affected their religious and cultural rights. All twelve sabhas consulted rejected it: a local sacred voice against a development metanarrative.

> **Answer line:** "Postmodernism empowers the marginalised when its suspicion of power is joined to a defence of rights."

## 8. The internal critique — feminists

- **Clifford excused the absence of feminists** from *Writing Culture*, arguing that feminist work changed content, not textual form.
- **Mascia-Lees, Sharpe & Cohen, "The Postmodernist Turn in Anthropology: Cautions from a Feminist Perspective" (*Signs*, 1989)** — feminism questioned objectivity first, and it kept a politics.
- **Behar & Gordon (eds.), *Women Writing Culture* (1995).**
- **Abu-Lughod (1991)** — feminists and "halfies" were already writing against culture.
- **Faye Harrison (ed.), *Decolonizing Anthropology* (1991)** — native and Third-World scholars had long been doing this work.

## 9. Criticism and legacy

| Criticism | Who |
|---|---|
| Anti-science, relativism | **Spiro 1996** (*Comparative Studies in Society and History*); D'Andrade 1995 |
| Obscurantism, idealism, neglect of material causes | **Harris, *Theories of Culture in Postmodern Times* (1999)** |
| Navel-gazing — the author replaces the people | Sangren 1988 (*Current Anthropology*) |
| Fashion; relativism as a dead end | Gellner, *Postmodernism, Reason and Religion* (1992) |
| Confuses moral advocacy with knowledge | D'Andrade, "Moral Models in Anthropology" (1995) |
| A critique of Western authority written by Western academics | Harrison 1991 |

**Legacy (the verdict):** ==it faded as a programme for writing and won as a sensibility==. Reflexivity, ethics, consent, collaboration, attention to voice and power, multi-sited fieldwork and the critique of bounded cultures are now ordinary anthropology.

## 10. Comparisons

- **Geertz vs postmodern**
  - Geertz is the **precursor**: culture as text, read *over the shoulders* of natives, with the interpreter's authority retained, integrated webs of significance, and little attention to power.
  - The postmodernists question that authority, treat culture as fragmented, and put power at the centre.
- **Lévi-Strauss vs Derrida**
  - Lévi-Strauss: stable, centred, universal structure; binaries mediated in myth; meaning fixed by position in the system.
  - Derrida: decentred free play; binaries are hierarchies to be reversed and displaced; meaning deferred (*différance*).
  - On writing, Lévi-Strauss ties it to domination (the Nambikwara lesson); Derrida says violence precedes writing.
- **Postmodernism vs Marxism on justice**
  - Postmodernism locates oppression in discourse and categories, works through plural identities and rejects metanarratives.
  - Marxism locates it in relations of production, works through a class, and is itself a metanarrative.
  - Their weaknesses: relativism and fragmentation for the first; neglect of caste, gender and culture for the second.

## The traps

1. **Deconstruction is not destruction** — it reverses and displaces.
2. **"Nothing outside the text" does not deny reality.**
3. **Geertz is a precursor**, not a postmodernist.
4. **Postmodernity (the condition) ≠ postmodernism (the theory).**
5. **"Critically" and "how relevant" stems need the limits** — never write a celebration.
6. **Attach anthropologists to each feature**, not only philosophers.
7. Clifford and Marcus disliked the label "postmodern"; textbooks also call it the **reflexive or literary turn**.
