## 1. How sex is set

- ==**SRY**, a gene on the Y chromosome, switches the early gonad into a **testis**.== Without SRY, the gonad becomes an ovary.
- ==**Rule: a Y means male development, however many X chromosomes there are.**==
  - So XXY is male.
  - XO and XXX are female.

## 2. Why extra X chromosomes are tolerated

🪝 *1949: **Barr** saw a dark spot in the nerve cells of female cats, never in males. It was the switched-off X — the **Barr body**.*

- ==**Lyon hypothesis (1961):** early in the embryo, all X chromosomes except one are randomly switched off in each cell.== Each switched-off X shows up as a Barr body.
- ==**Number of Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes − 1.**==
  - XY, XO → 0 Barr bodies
  - XX, XXY → 1
  - XXX → 2
- 🪝 *Calico cats are almost always female: each fur patch has a different X switched off.*
- **Why sex-chromosome errors are milder than autosomal ones:**
  - extra X chromosomes are silenced;
  - the Y carries only a few dozen genes.
  - So the effects fall mainly on **growth, puberty and fertility**.

## 3. How these conditions arise (2020)

Non-disjunction — the failure of chromosomes to separate when eggs or sperm are made.

| Abnormal egg or sperm | + normal one | Child |
|---|---|---|
| egg XX | sperm Y | **XXY** (Klinefelter) |
| sperm XY (father, first meiotic division) | egg X | **XXY** |
| egg XX | sperm X | **XXX** |
| sperm YY (father, second meiotic division) | egg X | **XYY** |
| sperm with no sex chromosome | egg X | ==**45,X** (Turner) — mostly from the father== |
| egg with no X | sperm Y | 45,Y — not viable; no life without an X |

**Mosaics (1995):** if the error happens after fertilisation, the person has two cell lines (e.g. 45,X/46,XX or 46,XY/47,XXY). Effects are **milder**, depending on what share of cells is affected and where. A 45,X/46,XY mosaic can give ambiguous development and a risk of tumours.

## 4. The four conditions

| | **Klinefelter** | **Turner** | **Triple X** | **XYY** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Karyotype | ==47,XXY== | ==45,X== | 47,XXX | 47,XYY |
| Sex | male | female | female | male |
| Barr bodies | 1 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Frequency | ~1 in 500–1,000 males | ~1 in 2,500 females | ~1 in 1,000 females | ~1 in 1,000 males |
| First described | 1942 (XXY found 1959) | 1938 (45,X found 1959) | — | Jacobs 1965 |

**Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)**
- tall, with long limbs
- small testes that produce no sperm, so the man is infertile
- breast growth
- sparse body hair
- mild learning or speech difficulty
- often discovered only at an infertility clinic
- treated with testosterone
- more extra Xs (48,XXXY) → more severe

**Turner syndrome (45,X)**
- ==the **only full monosomy** a person survives — about 99% of 45,X pregnancies miscarry==
- **mostly comes from the father**, so it is **not linked to the mother's age**
- swollen hands and feet at birth
- short stature
- webbed neck and broad chest
- **streak ovaries**, so no puberty and infertility
- heart (aorta) and kidney defects
- intelligence usually normal
- about half are mosaics or have an abnormal X
- treated with growth hormone and oestrogen

**Triple X (47,XXX)**
- the old label "**super female**" is a myth — there is nothing "super" about it
- often never noticed
- tall, with mild learning difficulty
- usually fertile

**XYY (47,XYY)**
- tall and usually fertile; often never diagnosed
- 🪝 *1965: **Jacobs** found XYY men in a Scottish secure hospital, and newspapers called it the "**criminal chromosome**". Later studies of the general population found most XYY men live ordinary lives.*

## 5. Klinefelter vs Turner — the favourite pair

| | Klinefelter | Turner |
|---|---|---|
| Karyotype | 47,XXY — an extra X | 45,X — a missing X |
| Sex / Barr bodies | male / 1 | female / 0 |
| Stature | tall | short |
| Gonads | small testes, no sperm | streak ovaries, no puberty |
| Other features | breast growth; sparse hair | webbed neck; heart and kidney defects |
| Mind | mild learning difficulty | usually normal; trouble with spatial tasks |
| Origin | either parent; linked to mother's age | mostly from the father; not age-linked |
| Usually found | at an infertility clinic | at birth (swelling) or from short stature |
| Treatment | testosterone | growth hormone; oestrogen |

## 6. Intersex — differences of sex development

🪝 *The **guevedoces** of the Dominican Republic appear to be girls at birth and become men at puberty. In 1974 the cause was found: they lack the enzyme **5-α-reductase**.*

- **Three levels of sex:** chromosomal (XX or XY) · gonadal (ovaries or testes) · bodily.
- ==**Intersex**, also called **DSD (differences of sex development)**: these three levels do not line up in the usual way.==

| Condition | Chromosomes | Gonads | Body at birth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Androgen insensitivity | XY | testes | female |
| 5-α-reductase deficiency | XY | testes | female, becoming male at puberty |
| Swyer syndrome | XY | streak gonads | female |
| Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) | XX | ovaries | masculinised |
| XX male (SRY moved onto an X) | XX | testes | male |
| Mosaic / ovotesticular | mixed | mixed | variable |

**Why it matters for anthropology**
- Biological sex is **not always either/or**, and how a society classifies people is partly cultural.
- India: the **hijra** community; ==the **NALSA judgment (2014)** recognised a third gender==; the **Transgender Persons Act 2019** followed.
- **Intersex ≠ transgender.** Intersex is about how the body develops; transgender is about gender identity.
- Sport used chromosome tests from the 1960s and later abandoned them. In 2015 **Dutee Chand** challenged the rules on naturally high testosterone.

## 7. Sex-chromatin tests

- **Buccal smear:** cells from inside the cheek, checked for Barr bodies.
- **Drumstick:** a small projection seen in white blood cells of people with more than one X.
- **Y-body:** a fluorescent spot marking the Y chromosome.
- These tests are cheap but miss mosaics, so they have been replaced by karyotyping, FISH and DNA tests.

## The traps
1. Klinefelter is **male** (has a Y); Turner is **female** (no Y).
2. Turner usually comes **from the father** and is **not linked to the mother's age**.
3. "**Super female**" and "**criminal chromosome**" are discredited labels — say so in the exam.
4. **Intersex ≠ transgender.**
