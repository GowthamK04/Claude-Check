## 1. Two kinds of force

Hardy–Weinberg (9.3-A) says a gene pool stays the same unless something acts on it. Six things do:

- ==**Four change how common an allele is:** mutation · migration · drift · selection.==
- ==**One changes only how alleles are paired:** **inbreeding** — it produces more homozygotes, but **does not change allele frequency**.==
- **One is a condition, not a force:** **isolation**. It blocks gene flow, which lets drift and selection work on each group separately.

> **Line:** ==mutation creates · migration mixes · drift and isolation divide · selection directs · inbreeding re-pairs.==

## 2. Mutation — the only source of new variation

🪝 *In 1901 **de Vries** saw evening primroses suddenly produce new forms that bred true. He named these sudden changes "mutations".*

- ==**Mutation:** a heritable change in DNA. It is the **only** way a new allele appears== — the raw material of evolution.
- **Three scales of mutation:**
  - **point** — one DNA letter changes. ==Sickle-cell: one changed letter swaps glutamic acid for valine in haemoglobin.==
  - **frameshift** — letters are added or lost, so everything after the change is scrambled.
  - **chromosomal** — large pieces are lost, copied, flipped or moved, or a whole chromosome is added (e.g. Down syndrome, 9.4).
- **Causes:** **spontaneous** (copying errors) or **induced** (radiation, chemicals).
- 🪝 *Each of us carries about 60–70 new mutations our parents did not have — most are harmless.*
- **Rate:** about 1 in 100,000 per gene per generation. So mutation on its own changes frequencies very slowly; its importance is the steady supply of new variation.
- ==**What mutation means for evolution (2019):**==
  1. it supplies the variation that selection acts on (lactase persistence, malaria resistance);
  2. most mutations are neutral or harmful, and the harmful ones build up as **genetic load** (9.3-C);
  3. neutral changes pile up at a steady rate, so they work as a **molecular clock** for dating when groups split;
  4. **mutation–selection balance** — new harmful mutations keep appearing while selection removes them, so some diseases stay at a low, steady level.

## 3. Migration (gene flow) — makes groups alike

- People who move bring their alleles with them, so ==gene flow makes populations **more alike**==. The effect is faster with more migrants and a bigger starting difference.
- **Indian examples:**
  - **Siddis** of Gujarat and Karnataka — African and Indian ancestry mixed over centuries;
  - **Parsis** — came from Iran and have stayed largely endogamous since;
  - most Indians — descend from an ancient mixing of several ancestral populations.

## 4. Genetic drift — the force of chance

🪝 *Toss a coin 10 times and you may get 7 heads. Toss it 10,000 times and you get very close to half. A small population is like the 10 tosses.*

- ==**Drift:** each generation passes on a **random sample** of the previous one's alleles, so in **small** groups allele frequencies wander — towards **loss** or **fixation** (only one version left).==
- Drift has **no direction**, so it can make even a harmful allele common.
- **Sewall Wright** (1930s) showed how powerful drift is in small, isolated groups (the "Sewall Wright effect").
- ==**Founder effect:** a few people leave to start a new group and carry only a chance sample of alleles.==
  - 🪝 *The Pennsylvania **Amish**: **McKusick** traced Ellis–van Creveld syndrome (short stature, extra fingers) back to one founding couple.*
  - 🪝 *A 2017 study found many Indian caste and tribal groups have **stronger founder effects than Finns** — for example, a variant common among the **Vysyas** of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana makes a widely used anaesthetic dangerous.*
- ==**Bottleneck:** most of a population dies, and the survivors' genes become the entire future gene pool, so variation is lost.== Example: the **Great Andamanese**, who fell to a few dozen people.

> **Rule:** ==drift rules small populations; selection rules large ones.==

## 5. Isolation — keeps groups apart

- **Geographical** isolation (mountains, islands) or **social** isolation (caste, tribe, religion, language).
- Isolation cuts gene flow, so drift and selection act separately in each group and the groups drift apart into **genetic isolates**.
- 🪝 *Toda of the Nilgiris (about 2,000 people); Onge and Jarawa of the Andamans — isolated both geographically and socially.*
- **India:** about 2,000 years of caste and tribal endogamy have produced thousands of partly separate gene pools — the Mendelian populations of 9.3-A.

## 6. Selection — the only force with a direction

- Covered in full in 9.3-A. It **directs** change:
  - **directional** — lactase persistence spreading;
  - **balancing** — sickle-cell kept common where there is malaria;
  - it also slowly removes harmful mutations.

## 7. Inbreeding — re-pairs alleles, doesn't change the pool

- Relatives share alleles, so their child may receive two copies from the same ancestor. The result is **more homozygotes**, which exposes hidden recessives.
- ==Inbreeding **does not change allele frequency**== (see 9.3-C).

## 8. Comparisons

| | Drift | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | chance | differences in fitness |
| Direction | random | towards what suits the environment |
| Strongest in | small groups | large groups |
| Effect on harmful alleles | can make them common | removes them (unless balanced) |

| | Founder effect | Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|
| How it starts | a few leave | most die |
| Original group | unchanged | reduced |
| Example | Amish; Indian castes | Great Andamanese |

**Migration vs isolation:** migration pulls gene pools together; isolation lets them drift apart. India's history shows both — ancient mixing, then thousands of years of endogamy.

## 9. The three names to know

| Name | Remember them for |
|---|---|
| **de Vries** (1901) | coined "mutation" |
| **Sewall Wright** (1930s) | drift in small populations |
| **McKusick** (1960s) | the Amish founder effect |

## The traps
1. **Inbreeding does not change allele frequency** — only genotype frequency.
2. **Drift is random; selection is directed.**
3. **Founder effect** (some leave) ≠ **bottleneck** (most die).
4. **Isolation** works through drift and selection — it is a condition, not a direct force.
