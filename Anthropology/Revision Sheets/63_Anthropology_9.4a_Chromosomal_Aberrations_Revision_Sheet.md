## 1. The normal set

🪝 *In 1923 Painter counted 48 human chromosomes, and textbooks repeated that number for 33 years. In 1956 Tjio and Levan counted correctly: 46.*

- ==**46 chromosomes = 23 pairs.**==
  - 22 pairs are **autosomes**, numbered 1–22 from largest to smallest.
  - 1 pair are the **sex chromosomes**: XX in females, XY in males.
- Eggs and sperm carry only 23, so fertilisation restores 46. A normal set is written **46,XX** or **46,XY**.
- The **centromere** is the narrow waist that splits each chromosome into a short **p** arm and a long **q** arm.
- Three shapes, by where the centromere sits:
  - **metacentric** — in the middle;
  - **submetacentric** — off-centre;
  - **acrocentric** — near one end. ==Chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22 and Y are acrocentric, and these are the ones that can fuse.==
- 🪝 *Apes have 48 chromosomes. Our chromosome 2 is two ape chromosomes fused end to end.*

## 2. Making a karyotype (2024)

A **karyotype** is a photograph of a cell's chromosomes, arranged in pairs.

Steps in making one:
1. **Sample** — blood (white cells), or before birth, fluid from amniocentesis or cells from chorionic villi.
2. **Culture** — the cells are stimulated to divide.
3. ==**Arrest with colchicine**== — this stops division at metaphase, when chromosomes are most compact and easiest to see.
4. ==**Spread with a weak salt solution**== — the cells swell and the chromosomes spread apart. This was Tjio and Levan's trick.
5. **Stain with Giemsa** — this produces light and dark bands (**G-banding**).
6. **Arrange** — pair the chromosomes by size, centromere position and banding.

**Notation:**
- 47,XY,+21 — Down syndrome
- 45,X — Turner syndrome
- 47,XXY — Klinefelter syndrome
- 46,XY,del(5p) — cri-du-chat
- t(9;22) — the Philadelphia chromosome

**What a karyotype helps diagnose:**
- numerical errors, visible at a glance
- large structural changes
- confirming a syndrome, and telling free Down syndrome from translocation Down
- prenatal diagnosis
- infertility and repeated miscarriage
- cancer
- radiation damage

**Finer tools:** FISH and microarray, which can find tiny changes a karyotype misses.

## 3. Numerical aberrations — the wrong number

**How a child ends up with 47 chromosomes:**
- In meiosis, each chromosome pair should split, one copy to each egg or sperm.
- If a pair **fails to separate** — ==**non-disjunction**== — one gamete gets 24 chromosomes (both copies) and another gets 22 (neither).
- When a normal gamete (23) fertilises them, the result is ==**47 (trisomy)** or **45 (monosomy)**.==

**Two kinds:**
- **Polyploidy** — whole extra sets (69 or 92 chromosomes). Lethal before birth.
- **Aneuploidy** — one chromosome extra or missing:
  - **autosomal trisomies:** Down (+21), Edwards (+18), Patau (+13). Only these survive because 13, 18 and 21 carry fewer genes; trisomies of bigger chromosomes end in miscarriage.
  - **sex-chromosome aneuploidies:** XXY, XXX, XYY, and 45,X. These are milder because extra X chromosomes are switched off and the Y has few genes (see 9.4b).
  - ==**Turner syndrome (45,X) is the only full monosomy a person can survive.**==

**Risk rises with the mother's age.** Down syndrome risk:

| Mother's age | Down syndrome risk |
|---|---|
| 20 | ~1 in 1,500 |
| 35 | ~1 in 350 |
| 40 | ~1 in 100 |
| 45 | ~1 in 30 |

- **Mosaics:** if the error happens after fertilisation, the person has two cell lines (some normal, some not), and the effects are milder.
- 🪝 *About half of all early miscarriages carry a chromosomal abnormality.*

## 4. Structural aberrations — the wrong arrangement (2018, 2023)

🪝 *1960: Nowell and Hungerford found the **Philadelphia chromosome** in leukaemia cells — a piece of chromosome 9 had swapped with a piece of chromosome 22. It was the first chromosome change ever linked to cancer.*

**Causes (2023):** structural changes start with a **break**, which then rejoins wrongly. Breaks are caused by:
- ionising radiation
- chemicals and drugs
- viruses
- inherited fragile sites
- errors in copying or crossing-over

**The letters model** — picture a normal chromosome as **A B C • D E F G** (• = centromere):

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Deletion** | a piece is lost: A B C • D _ F G | ==cri-du-chat (5p−)== |
| **Duplication** | a piece is doubled: … D E E F G | usually milder |
| **Inversion** | a piece is flipped: … D F E G (pericentric if it includes the centromere, paracentric if not) | the carrier is usually healthy |
| **Reciprocal translocation** | two chromosomes swap pieces | ==Philadelphia t(9;22)== |
| **Robertsonian translocation** | two acrocentric chromosomes fuse at the centromere | ==familial Down syndrome t(14;21) — inherited, not linked to mother's age== |
| **Ring / isochromosome** | the ends join into a ring / one arm is doubled | i(Xq) in some Turner cases |

==A **balanced** rearrangement loses nothing, so the carrier is healthy — but their children can inherit an unbalanced set.==

## 5. Why aberrations cause havoc (2021)

- ==**Gene dosage:**== an extra chromosome raises the dose of hundreds of genes by 50%; a missing one cuts it by 50%. Development depends on genes working in exact proportions.
- **Effects on the body:** heart defects, unusual stature, facial features, low muscle tone, kidney and gut problems, infertility.
- **Effects on the mind:** intellectual disability, and difficulties with learning, speech or behaviour.
- **Rules of thumb:**
  - losing material is worse than gaining it;
  - autosomal errors are worse than sex-chromosome errors;
  - balanced rearrangements may cause no harm at all.
- 🪝 *About 1 in 150 babies has a chromosomal abnormality — in India, that is a lakh or more every year.*

**India:**
- Later motherhood raises the risk.
- Consanguinity does **not** raise most chromosomal disorders, because they arise fresh in each pregnancy.
- The **PCPNDT Act 1994** bans using prenatal tests for sex selection.
- Studies on Kerala's high-background-radiation coast have found no clear rise in chromosomal disorders.

## The traps
1. **Turner syndrome** is the only viable full monosomy.
2. **Translocation Down** is inherited and **not** linked to the mother's age.
3. **Polyploidy** (whole sets) ≠ **aneuploidy** (single chromosomes).
4. Only **acrocentric** chromosomes form Robertsonian translocations.
