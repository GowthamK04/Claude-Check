## 1. Why the Mesolithic began

- The **Holocene** began about **11,700 years ago**. The monsoon grew stronger, the climate warmer, and forests and lakes spread.
- So people faced **smaller, faster game**, plus fish and plant foods.
- The Mesolithic runs from about ==**12,000 to 6,000 years ago**== (later in some regions).

**How people adapted (2001):**
1. a **broad-spectrum diet** — eating many kinds of food;
2. **composite tools** (made of several parts) suited to small game;
3. **longer stays** where food was plentiful;
4. the **first taming of animals and processing of plants**.

## 2. Microliths

🪝 *A single arrow carried several microliths: one at the tip and others glued along the shaft as barbs. If one chipped, you replaced just that piece.*

- **Microliths** are tiny stone tools, usually under about 5 cm, made of chert, chalcedony, agate or quartz.
- ==They were **hafted** (set into handles) with resin to make arrows, harpoons and sickles.==
- Two kinds:
  - ==**Non-geometric** (earlier):== blade, backed blade, point, scraper, borer.
  - ==**Geometric** (later):== triangle, trapeze, lunate (crescent), rhomboid.
- Later sites also have querns (grinding stones), ring stones, bone tools and hand-made pottery.

## 3. Regional variations (2013, 2018)

| Region | Sites | What is distinctive |
|---|---|---|
| **West** | ==**Bagor** (V. N. Misra), **Langhnaj** (Sankalia)==, Tilwara | ==early domestic **sheep and goats** at Bagor, ~5000 BC==; crouched burials at Langhnaj |
| **Central** | **Bhimbetka**, **Adamgarh**, Jaora | rock art; early domestic animals at Adamgarh (debated) |
| **Ganga plain** | ==**Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Damdama**== (G. R. Sharma) | camps beside ox-bow lakes; many burials; tall, robust people |
| **Vindhyas** | ==**Chopani-Mando**== (Belan valley) | a full sequence at one site; pottery and wild rice |
| **East** | Birbhanpur, Chota Nagpur sites | mostly non-geometric microliths, on laterite soils |
| **South** | **Teri** sites, Sanganakallu | red coastal sand dunes; quartz microliths |

> **Line:** ==the Indian Mesolithic was many regional cultures, not one.==

## 4. The Ganga basin (2026)

🪝 *Mahadaha means "great lake". Its people camped by an ox-bow lake and buried their dead inside the camp — sometimes two in one grave.*

**The setting:** the sites are in Pratapgarh district, Uttar Pradesh, beside **ox-bow lakes** (curved lakes left behind when a river changes course). The lakes were rich in fish, turtles and birds, and drew game.

**What was found:**
- **Artefacts:**
  - microliths
  - bone and antler tools
  - bone ornaments
  - querns
  - clay-lined hearths
  - ==**hut floors and storage pits**==
- **Animal bones:** wild cattle, buffalo, chital, sambar, barasingha, pig, rhino, elephant, fish and turtle — ==**no firm evidence of domestication**==.
- **Human skeletons:**
  - ==**dozens of burials** laid out straight **inside the camp**, some with more than one person==
  - grave goods buried with them
  - **tall, robust**, long-headed people
  - some healed injuries

**What it means:** ==people lived semi-settled lives **without farming**.== But the dates are debated and plant evidence is thin.

## 5. The Belan valley (2016)

**Chopani-Mando** shows the whole sequence in one place:
1. late Upper Palaeolithic
2. early Mesolithic (non-geometric microliths)
3. ==advanced Mesolithic (geometric microliths, **huts, querns, hand-made pottery, wild rice**)==
4. the Neolithic nearby, at Koldihwa and Mahagara

## 6. Rock art (2015, 2023)

🪝 *1957: from a train window, **Wakankar** noticed odd rock formations near Bhopal. He got off and found **Bhimbetka**.*

- ==**Bhimbetka:** more than 700 rock shelters, about 400 of them painted; UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003.==
- **Mesolithic scenes:** hunting with bows, dancing, honey collection, women gathering; bison, deer, boar, rhino.
- **Colours:** mainly red and white, from minerals such as haematite.
- Similar scenes survive in central Indian tribal life today.
- Other shelters: Jaora, Adamgarh, Raisen, Pachmarhi.
- 🪝 ***Lakhudiyar** near Almora — the name is said to mean "a lakh caves" — shows figures holding hands in a line dance, animals and wavy lines (the 2023 Uttarakhand note).*

## 7. A step towards settled life (2009, 2023)

**Was the Mesolithic the first step? Largely yes.** The evidence:
- hut floors, post-holes, hearths and storage pits (Ganga plain, Belan)
- burials inside the settlement (Mahadaha, Damdama)
- querns and wild rice (Chopani-Mando)
- crude pottery in the late levels
- ==early herding (Bagor, Adamgarh)==

**What it gave the Neolithic:** grinding, storage, pottery, herding, and the habit of staying in one place.

**The other side:** the change was gradual, varied by region, and could reverse. Many groups stayed mobile, and hunter-gatherers lived alongside farmers for thousands of years (see 1.3).

## 8. V. N. Misra (2020)

- **1935–2015**, Deccan College, Pune.
- Excavated **Bagor** (from 1967): one of India's longest Mesolithic sequences, with early domestic sheep and goats.
- Worked out the **Bhimbetka** sequence with Wakankar.
- Studied the **Didwana** dunes.
- A **pioneer of ethnoarchaeology** in India.

## 9. Upper Palaeolithic vs Mesolithic

| | Upper Palaeolithic | Mesolithic |
|---|---|---|
| Climate | cold, dry | warm, wet |
| Tools | blades | microliths |
| Food | big game | many kinds of food |
| Settlement | mobile | longer stays, huts |
| Burial and art | rare | common |

**West vs Ganga plain:** the west shows mobile dune camps moving towards **herding**; the Ganga plain shows **settled lake-side foragers who did not herd**.

## The traps
1. Bhimbetka spans many periods; its richest paintings are Mesolithic.
2. The Ganga sites show **no** domestication; Bagor and Adamgarh do.
3. Non-geometric microliths are **earlier** than geometric ones.
4. Koldihwa and Mahagara are **Neolithic** (see 1.1.c).
