## 1. Discovery

🪝 *30 May 1863: **Robert Bruce Foote** picked up a handaxe in a gravel pit at **Pallavaram**, near Madras. Indian prehistory began there.* Sixty km away, **Attirampakkam** has handaxes dated to about **1.5 million years** — among the oldest outside Africa.

- ==**Foote** (Geological Survey of India) — the father of Indian prehistory.==
- ==**de Terra & Paterson (1935)** — studied the Soan river terraces in the Potwar plateau.==
- ==**Sankalia (1950s)** — excavated Nevasa and defined the Middle Palaeolithic.==
- The Upper Palaeolithic was accepted only from the 1960s–70s.

## 2. The three stages

| | **Lower** | **Middle** | **Upper** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dates | ~1.5 Ma – 200 ka | ~385 ka – 40 ka | ~45 ka – 12 ka |
| How tools were made | big cores, flaked on both faces | flakes struck from prepared (Levallois) cores | long blades from prepared cores |
| Tools | handaxe, cleaver, chopper | scraper, point, borer | blade, burin, backed blade; bone tools |
| Stone | quartzite | chert, jasper, agate | finer stones, wider range |
| Key sites | Attirampakkam, Isampur, Didwana, Bhimbetka, Narmada, Soan | Nevasa, Attirampakkam, Jwalapuram | Patne, Kurnool caves, Renigunta, Baghor I, Belan |
| Makers | Homo erectus-like hominins | debated | Homo sapiens |

> **Line:** ==big cores became prepared flakes, and flakes became slim blades.==

## 3. Soan vs Madras — the "two traditions" debate

**The old view:**
- **Soan** (north-west, Potwar plateau) = pebble **choppers** and **chopping tools**, in two phases: early (large, worn pebbles) → late (more flakes). Seen as linked to East Asia.
- **Madras** (south) = **handaxes and cleavers** (the Acheulian tradition). Seen as linked to Africa and Europe.

**Why the theory fell:**
- handaxes were later found in the Siwalik and Soan region;
- pebble tools turned up in the south;
- the Soan terraces proved hard to date.

==**Today: one Lower Palaeolithic complex with regional variants; the Soan is a regional pebble-tool industry.**==

## 4. Lower Palaeolithic

🪝 *At **Isampur** (Hunsgi valley, Karnataka), half-finished handaxes and thousands of flakes still lie where people sat and worked — a factory floor over a million years old.*

- **Sites:** ==Attirampakkam (~1.5 Ma)== · Isampur and the Hunsgi–Baichbal valleys (>1 Ma) · Chirki-Nevasa · Bhimbetka · the Narmada · Didwana · Paisra · the Soan.
- People camped in the open, near rivers. Their tools are often found in river gravels.

## 5. Middle Palaeolithic

🪝 *74,000 years ago the **Toba** volcano in Sumatra erupted and its ash fell on India. At **Jwalapuram** (Andhra Pradesh), stone tools lie both below and above the ash — people survived.*

- ==**Prepared-core flakes**== were shaped into scrapers, points and borers, using finer stones.
- **Nevasa** (on the Pravara river) — Sankalia's type site, so the stage was once called "Nevasian" or "Series II".
- ==**Attirampakkam ~385,000 years** (dated in 2018)== — much earlier than expected.
- Other sites: Bhimbetka · Didwana · the Son · the Luni.
- **Who made these tools is debated.**

## 6. Upper Palaeolithic

🪝 *At **Patne** (Maharashtra), about 25,000 years ago, someone engraved criss-cross lines on ostrich eggshell and made beads from it — India's earliest art.*

- **Blade:** a flake at least twice as long as it is wide, with parallel sides. Blades were made into burins, backed blades, scrapers and points.
- ==This stage brings the **first bone tools, ornaments and art**==, and people also used caves and rock shelters.

**Sites by region:**
- **South:** Renigunta, Yerragondapalem; ==**Kurnool caves** (bone tools)==; the Shorapur doab.
- **Deccan:** ==**Patne** (engraved eggshell, beads)==; Inamgaon.
- **Central:** Bhimbetka; the Son valley — ==**Baghor I**, a stone interpreted as an early shrine==; the Belan valley.
- **West:** Visadi (Gujarat); the Thar.

**Tool traditions:** some sites have a true blade-and-burin industry (Renigunta), others mix flakes and blades, and some already show small tools that lead into the microliths of the Mesolithic. ==There is **continuity, not a sharp break**.==

## 7. Environment (2023)

- The climate swung with the **monsoon**:
  - **dry phases** — the Thar spread and dunes built up (Didwana);
  - **wet phases** — rivers ran full.
- **Evidence:**
  - river terraces and gravels
  - sand dunes
  - fossil animals (elephant, hippo, wild cattle — Narmada, Son)
  - pollen
  - ==the **Toba ash** (74,000 years ago)==

## 8. Why the Indian Palaeolithic is not homogeneous (2013, 2019)

1. **The stages overlap in time.** Handaxes appear 1.5 million years ago at Attirampakkam but last to about 150,000 years ago elsewhere. Middle Palaeolithic tools appear by 385,000 years ago while handaxes continue in other places.
2. **Regions differ** — different river basins, plateaus and local stone.
3. **There is continuity** — flake-making runs through all stages, and the Upper Palaeolithic shades into the Mesolithic.
4. **Dating is weak** — many finds are surface finds or were washed into gravels (secondary context); there are few absolute dates; and dating by river terraces failed.
5. **The terms are borrowed** — "Acheulian" and "Levallois" come from Europe.
6. **There are almost no human fossils** — only the Narmada skull (see 1.2).

## 9. South Indian palaeoliths (2015, 2024)

**Sites:**
- Pallavaram
- ==Attirampakkam (1.5 Ma for handaxes; 385 ka for Middle Palaeolithic)==
- Isampur and Hunsgi
- Jwalapuram
- Renigunta and the Kurnool caves
- Krishna–Godavari sites

**Why they matter:**
- they are among the earliest sites outside Africa;
- new technologies arrived early here;
- they are the best-dated sites in India;
- all three stages are present;
- it is where Indian prehistory began.

## 10. Foote (2020)

- **1834–1912**, Geological Survey of India.
- Found Pallavaram (1863) and Attirampakkam.
- Recorded hundreds of sites across the Madras Presidency, Hyderabad and the Deccan.
- Excavated the Kurnool caves (Billa Surgam) with his son in the 1880s.
- His collection is in the Chennai museum.
- Called the **father of Indian prehistory**.

## The traps
1. The Soan is in the Potwar plateau (Pakistan), not the Narmada valley.
2. A **chopper** is flaked on one face; a **chopping tool** on both.
3. Bhimbetka's famous paintings are mostly **Mesolithic** (see 1.1.b).
4. The makers of Middle Palaeolithic tools are **debated** — don't state them as certain.
